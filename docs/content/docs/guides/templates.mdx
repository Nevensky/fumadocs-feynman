A template draws a standard diagram from one line. `fock` is the Fock exchange self-energy: two vertices on the
electron line, the bare Coulomb line v between them, and the two external legs.

```feynman source
fock
```

A template stands for statements you could write yourself, and the **By hand** tab under the diagram shows them:
the vertices, then the lines. Use the template for the diagram it draws, and its By hand form as the start of one
that no template draws.

## Writing a template

A template statement is the template's name, then its options:

```feynman source
ladder n=3 pp rung=phonon rungLabel=F
```

| You write | It sets | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `key=value` | one option | `n=3`, `rung=phonon`, `G="m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}"` |
| a flag | the options it stands for | `pp` is `channel=pp`; in `exciton`, `charge` is `vertex=charge ext=screened extLabel=W` |
| the name of an on/off option | that option, on | `dressed`, `vertical`, `crossed` |
| a quoted string | the `label` option, in the templates that have one | `propagator "G"`, `bubble "P"` |

- Put text that has spaces or TeX in double quotes, as in `label="\Sigma^{ep}"`. A plain word or a number needs none.
- `=false` turns off an option that is on by default: `gw dressed=false`, `gt spin=false`.
- Each option has the type of its default. A number option takes a number after `=`: `ladder n=x` and a bare
  `ladder n` are errors (`ladder: n expects a number, as in n=3`). A text option needs a value: `ladder rung` is an
  error (`ladder: rung expects a value, as in rung=...`).
- Options and flags apply from left to right, so a flag can undo an option written before it:
  `exciton extLabel="W^T" charge` ends with `extLabel=W`, because `charge` sets it. Write flags first.

The [template reference](/docs/reference/templates) lists every template with its options, their defaults and its
flags.

## Errors in a template statement

An option that the template does not have is an error that suggests the nearest option and lists the ones there
are, and a misspelt template name gets the nearest template suggested: [Errors](/docs/guides/errors#unknown-option)
shows these messages.

Templates also check the values they are given, and the options that only work together, and say what they
expect:

```text
gt: form=round: the forms are arch, box and ladder
fock: inner= needs vertical: the inner diagram sits on the straight interaction of the standing self-energy
nbody: rungs: "14" needs two different lines between 1 and 2
```

A fumadocs build reports each error with its line in the `.mdx` file, and the page shows the errors where the
diagram would be. With `strict: true`, the build reports every error and then fails. See
[Errors](/docs/guides/errors).

## Adding statements to a template

A template that draws one diagram can share its term with statements of your own, written on the lines after it
(up to the next line that starts with an operator) or after a `;` on the same line. Its vertices keep their names,
so these statements can label a vertex, change its shape, and attach lines to it.

### The names of the vertices

Add `grid` on a line of its own to see the names: it draws the unit grid under every term of the block, and writes
the name of every vertex on top, where a lens or a blob cannot hide it. The By hand tab lists them too.

```feynman source
grid
fock vertical inner=chi
| vertex-correction ext=photon rung=phonon rungLabel=F
```

The names that come up most often:

| Name | Vertex |
| --- | --- |
| `a`, `b` | The two vertices of `fan-migdal`, `fock` and `gw`: `a` on the left and `b` on the right, or `a` below and `b` above with `vertical`. In `hartree`, `a` is on the electron line and `b` carries the loop. |
| `c`, `d`, `X` | With `inner=`: where the interaction meets the inner diagram, `c` below and `d` above, and the lens `X` of `inner=chi`. |
| `v` | The vertex of `debye-waller`, and the vertex of `vertex-correction` where the external line arrives. |
| `Gam` | Hedin's vertex triangle Γ, in `gw gamma=` and `bubble gamma=`. |
| `S` | The blob of `sigma`. |
| `v0` to `v3` | The vertices along the electron line of `sigma2`, from the left. |
| `v1`, `v2`, … | The vertices of `arcs`, numbered as in its `above=` and `below=`. |
| `a0`, `b0`, `a1`, `b1`, … | The left and right vertex of each bubble of `bubble`, `rpa` and `exciton`, counted from 0. `bubble gamma=` has `a` and `Gam`. |
| `t1`, `u1`, … | The upper and lower ends of the rungs of `ladder` and `crossed`. |
| `A`, `B` | The ends of the rung of `vertex-correction`, upper and lower; `A1`, `B1`, … with two rungs or more. |
| `K` | The box of `box`. |
| `v1_1`, `v2_1`, … | The rung ends of `nbody`, `trion` and `biexciton`: the line, then the column. |

The others, such as the rung ends inside the bubbles of `bubble`, `rpa` and `exciton` (`t0_1`, `u0_1`) or inside
`inner=bubble` (`l1`, `r1`), and the vertices of `gt`, are on the By hand tab.

<Callout type="warn">
A template's vertex replaces a vertex of the same name written before it, and `a at x,y` written after the template
replaces the template's `a`, shape and label included. Write your own vertices after the template, under names it
does not use, and change a template's vertex by its name alone, as below.
</Callout>

## Labelling and restyling vertices

A statement that starts with the name of a vertex changes that vertex in place. It takes a label, a shape and
vertex options such as `pos=` and `color=`; the vertex keeps its position and its lines. The label goes in double
quotes, and `pos=` puts it `below`, `above`, `left`, `right`, at a corner (`ne`, `nw`, `se`, `sw`) or at an angle.
Here the vertices of the exchange self-energy carry the plane-wave matrix elements ρ of the density:

```feynman source
fock vertical G="m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" v="v(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})"
a "\rho_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G})" pos=below
b "\rho^*_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G})" pos=above
```

A shape word changes the kind of vertex. In `vertex-correction`, the option `vertex=` sets the vertex `v` where the
external line arrives, but the ends of the rung have no option of their own, so they are restyled by name. The
photon couples to the current at `v` (teal), and the phonon to the electron at `A` and `B` (amber):

```feynman source
vertex-correction ext=photon rung=phonon rungLabel=F
v current
A eph
B eph
```

The [language reference](/docs/reference/language) lists the vertex shapes and options.

## Attaching more lines

Lines and legs can start or end at a template's vertices, as at your own. Here `ext=none` leaves out the external
lines of a chain of two bubbles, and a Coulomb line written by hand closes the chain into a ring: the second-order
ring diagram of the correlation energy, the lowest of the rings that the RPA sums.

```feynman source
rpa n=2 ext=none
b1 -- a0 coulomb bulge=1.8 below "v"
```

`leg` adds an external line to a template's vertex in the same way, as in `leg a0 left photon`.

## Templates in equations

A line that starts with an operator (`=`, `+`, `−`, …) begins the next term of an equation. A term can be
statements alone, a template, or a template with statements of your own. Here the Hartree–Fock self-energy, a blob
drawn by hand, is the sum of `hartree` and of `fock` with its vertices labelled r and r′:

```feynman source
S at (0, 0) blob "\Sigma^{HF}"
leg S left 0.95; leg S right 0.95
= hartree
+ fock
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=below
```

A line that starts with `|` sets the next diagram beside the last with no operator, and `\\` at the start of a line
begins a new row; see [Writing diagrams](/docs/guides/writing-diagrams). The templates that draw an equation
themselves (`dyson`, `hedin-vertex`, `bse`, `tmatrix`, `parquet` and `gt-bse`) must stand alone in their term, but
operators can join them to other terms.

## Direction and mirror images

The templates in which a particle runs one way take `dir=left`, which runs it from right to left: `propagator`,
`dyson`, `fan-migdal`, `fock`, `gw`, `sigma2`, `arcs`, `gt`, `gt-bse`, `nbody`, `trion` and `biexciton`. Mostly
this turns the arrows round and leaves the picture as it is. Where the two ends of a diagram differ, `dir=left`
draws the mirror image instead, so that the electron still enters through the same part. In `gw gamma=` the
electron enters through Hedin's vertex Γ, which moves to the right:

```feynman source
gw gamma="\Gamma"
| gw gamma="\Gamma" dir=left
```

`gt` and `gt-bse` work the same way. They follow the figures of Müller, Blügel and Friedrich, PRB 100, 045130 (2019),
with `dir=left` ([GT](/docs/guides/many-body#gt)), and the default, `dir=right`, is the mirror image. A momentum
arrow drawn with `mom=` follows the electron: it still points towards the vertex where the electron enters. See
[Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum).

## The By hand tab and expand()

With the word `source` in its fence, a block that uses a template shows its source in two tabs, as every diagram on
this page does: **Template**, the block as written, and **By hand**, the same diagram written out vertex by vertex
and line by line. `expand()` from the package returns the By hand form, and `render(expand(src))` draws the same
picture as `render(src)`. So the By hand form is a starting point when no template quite fits: copy it and change
what you need. The vertices keep their names, and the points that a template makes up, such as the free ends of its
legs, are written as coordinates.

[Source tabs](/docs/guides/source-tabs) shows how to turn the tabs on for every block, what By hand contains and how
to call `expand()` in code, and the [API reference](/docs/reference/api#expand) gives its signature.

## All templates

Each name links to the template's options and an example in the [template reference](/docs/reference/templates).

### Propagators and Dyson equations

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`propagator`](/docs/reference/templates#propagator) | One line of any style. |
| [`dyson`](/docs/reference/templates#dyson) | A Dyson equation: full = bare + bare · self-energy · full. |
| [`sigma`](/docs/reference/templates#sigma) | A self-energy blob with two stubs. |

[Dyson equations](/docs/examples/dyson-equations) has examples for the electron, the phonon and the photon.

### Self-energies

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`fan-migdal`](/docs/reference/templates#fan-migdal) | The Fan–Migdal self-energy: an electron line spanned by a phonon. |
| [`debye-waller`](/docs/reference/templates#debye-waller) | The Debye–Waller self-energy: a phonon loop on one vertex. |
| [`hartree`](/docs/reference/templates#hartree) | The Hartree tadpole. |
| [`fock`](/docs/reference/templates#fock) | The Fock exchange self-energy. |
| [`gw`](/docs/reference/templates#gw) | The GW self-energy; `gamma=` adds Hedin's vertex. |
| [`sigma2`](/docs/reference/templates#sigma2) | A second-order self-energy with two boson lines: rainbow, crossed or sequential. |
| [`arcs`](/docs/reference/templates#arcs) | Interaction arcs above and below one electron line, such as the exchange diagrams of Hedin's expansion. |

[Self-energies](/docs/guides/self-energies) covers them, standing up and with a diagram inside the interaction, and
[Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum) the momentum arrow beside the interaction.

### Polarization, screening and excitons

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`bubble`](/docs/reference/templates#bubble) | The polarization bubble; `gamma=` closes it with Hedin's vertex. |
| [`rpa`](/docs/reference/templates#rpa) | A chain of bubbles joined by interaction lines. |
| [`exciton`](/docs/reference/templates#exciton) | A bubble with a ladder of screened interactions inside. |

### Ladders and vertices

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`ladder`](/docs/reference/templates#ladder) | Two lines exchanging n interactions, particle–hole or particle–particle. |
| [`crossed`](/docs/reference/templates#crossed) | The maximally crossed ladder (Cooperon). |
| [`box`](/docs/reference/templates#box) | A four-point vertex with four legs. |
| [`vertex-correction`](/docs/reference/templates#vertex-correction) | Interactions across a three-point vertex, as a ladder or crossed. |
| [`hedin-vertex`](/docs/reference/templates#hedin-vertex) | Hedin's vertex equation, Γ = 1 + (δΣ/δG) GGΓ. |

### Two-particle equations

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`bse`](/docs/reference/templates#bse) | The Bethe–Salpeter equation, L = L⁰ + L⁰KL. |
| [`tmatrix`](/docs/reference/templates#tmatrix) | The T-matrix equation, T = V + VGGT. |
| [`parquet`](/docs/reference/templates#parquet) | The parquet decomposition of the full vertex F. |

### GT

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`gt`](/docs/reference/templates#gt) | The GT self-energy: a T matrix closed by G, drawn like GW, as a box or as a ladder. |
| [`gt-bse`](/docs/reference/templates#gt-bse) | GT with the T matrix in the ladder approximation. |

### Three and four particles

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`nbody`](/docs/reference/templates#nbody) | A ladder of any number of electron and hole lines, with rungs between any two. |
| [`trion`](/docs/reference/templates#trion) | Two electrons and a hole, or two holes and an electron. |
| [`biexciton`](/docs/reference/templates#biexciton) | Two electrons and two holes. |

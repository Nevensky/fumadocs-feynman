---
title: Self-energies
description: Templates for the electron self-energy, from the Σ blob to GWΓ and Hedin's expansion, lying down and standing up.
---

A self-energy Σ is what the interactions add to a propagator. The full electron propagator G follows from the
free one, G⁰, through the Dyson equation G = G⁰ + G⁰ΣG, and Σ is the sum of the diagrams that stay in one piece
when a single electron line is cut. The templates on this page draw these diagrams, each from one line. A template
combines with `=`, `+` and `|` into equations, and its vertices keep their names, so you can label them
afterwards. The [template reference](/docs/reference/templates) lists every option with its default.

| Template | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| [`sigma`](/docs/reference/templates#sigma) | Σ as a blob |
| [`fan-migdal`](/docs/reference/templates#fan-migdal), [`debye-waller`](/docs/reference/templates#debye-waller) | the electron–phonon self-energy |
| [`hartree`](/docs/reference/templates#hartree), [`fock`](/docs/reference/templates#fock), [`gw`](/docs/reference/templates#gw) | the first-order terms in v, and GW; `gw gamma=` adds Hedin's vertex |
| [`sigma2`](/docs/reference/templates#sigma2) | two boson lines on one electron line: rainbow, crossed, sequential |
| [`arcs`](/docs/reference/templates#arcs) | any number of interaction lines on one electron line, such as the exchange diagrams of Hedin's expansion |

## Σ as a blob

`sigma` draws Σ as a blob with two stubs, for an equation in which Σ stands for the whole sum. `label` names it
(`\Sigma` by default), `line` sets the style of the stubs and `r` the radius of the blob in units (0.45). The
next example puts it on the left of an equation.

## Electron–phonon: Fan–Migdal and Debye–Waller

To second order in the atomic displacements, the electron–phonon self-energy has two diagrams. In the
Fan–Migdal diagram the electron emits a phonon at one vertex and absorbs it again at another, and propagates in
between. In the Debye–Waller diagram a single second-order vertex emits and absorbs the phonon, so the phonon
line closes on itself and the term does not depend on frequency.

```feynman source
sigma label="\Sigma^{ep}"
= fan-migdal dressed vertex=disk
+ debye-waller dressed
```

`fan-migdal` draws the electron line from `a` to `b`, labelled G⁰ under it, and the phonon arc F⁰ over it.
`dressed` turns both into full propagators, labelled G and F; `G=` and `F=` set other labels. `vertex=` sets the
shape of `a` and `b` (here `disk`, a screened vertex), `phonon=` the style of the arc, and `width` the distance
from `a` to `b` (2.8 units). `debye-waller` draws the hatched vertex `v`, labelled `g^{DW}` (`vertexLabel=`),
with the phonon loop F⁰ on it, or F with `dressed`.

## Hartree, Fock and GW

To first order in the Coulomb interaction v there are two diagrams. In the Hartree diagram the electron feels the
electrostatic potential of the average electron density: a loop of G, closed on itself, joined to the electron
line by v. The Fock diagram is exchange, Σ = iGv: v spans the electron line. GW spans it with the screened
interaction W instead, Σ = iGW, the lowest order of Hedin's expansion in W.

```feynman source
hartree
caption "Hartree"
| fock
caption "Fock"
| gw
caption "GW"
```

`hartree` and `fock` draw the free G⁰ and the bare v (a dashed line); `gw` draws the full G (a double line) and W
(a double dashed line). `G=`, `v=` and `W=` set the labels, and `legLabel` labels the external electron. `fock`
and `gw` take `vertex=`, `width` and `dir`, as `fan-migdal` does, and their vertices are `a` and `b`. In
`hartree`, `a` is on the electron line and `b` at the foot of the loop, and `interaction=` sets the style of the
line between them (`coulomb`).

## Hedin's vertex: Σ = iGWΓ

Hedin's vertex function Γ corrects the vertex at which the electron enters: Σ = iGWΓ, and Γ = 1 gives GW back.
`gw gamma="\Gamma"` draws Γ as a triangle on the electron line where the electron enters, with W arriving at its
tip; the electron leaves at the bare vertex `b`.

```feynman source
gw gamma="\Gamma"
caption "\Sigma = iGW\Gamma"
| gw gamma="\Gamma" dir=left
caption "dir=left"
```

The triangle is the vertex `Gam`, coloured with `gcolor=` (`c4`, teal). With `dir=left` the whole diagram is
mirrored, so that Γ stays where the electron enters. `gamma=` does not stand up: with `vertical` it is an error.
`bubble gamma=` closes a polarization bubble with Γ in the same way, P = −iGGΓ; the
[vertex corrections examples](/docs/examples/vertex-corrections) show the two side by side.

## Second order: rainbow, crossed, sequential

Two boson lines on one electron line can sit in three ways, and `sigma2` draws each of them, with phonons unless
`boson=` names another style:

```feynman source
sigma2 rainbow
caption "rainbow"
| sigma2 crossed
caption "crossed"
| sigma2 sequential
caption "sequential"
```

- In the **rainbow** one phonon sits inside the other: it is a self-energy on the inner electron line. A full G
  holds it already, so `fan-migdal dressed` contains every rainbow.
- The **crossed** diagram is the first vertex correction. For phonons, Migdal's theorem makes it smaller than the
  rainbow by a factor of order √(m/M), the ratio of the phonon to the electron energy scale.
- The **sequential** diagram is two first-order diagrams in a row. Cutting the electron line between them
  separates them, so it is not part of Σ: the Dyson equation produces it from the first-order Σ.

`type=` takes the same three names as the flags (`crossed` by default). `dressed` dresses every line, `label`
names the boson line that starts at the first vertex, and `dir=left` turns the electron round. The vertices are
`v0` to `v3`, from the left.

## Hedin's expansion: arcs

`arcs` draws one electron line with interaction lines above and below it. It numbers the vertices 1, 2, … from
the left and takes each interaction as a pair of them, two digits such as `15` (or `1-5`):
`arcs above="15 24" below="36"` joins 1 to 5 and 2 to 4 above the line, and 3 to 6 below it. Each vertex ends
exactly one arc, and the numbers run from 1 with none left out; anything else is an error. Arcs on one side that
cross pass over one another. With neither `above=` nor `below=`, `arcs` draws `above="13 24"`, the second-order
exchange diagram.

Iterating Hedin's equations from Σ = iGW gives, at second order in W, the second-order screened exchange, the first
vertex correction. At third order come four exchange diagrams of this kind. Expanded in the full G and W, as
here with `dressed`, no diagram carries a self-energy insertion, so there is no rainbow among them. This is
Fig. 1 of Müller, Blügel and Friedrich, PRB 100, 045130 (2019), drawn as in the paper with the electron running
right to left, without its two direct third-order diagrams:

```feynman source
gw G="" W="" dir=left
| arcs above="13 24" dressed dir=left
\\ arcs above="15 24" below="36" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="13 46" below="25" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="26 35" below="14" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="14 36" below="25" dressed dir=left
```

The direct diagrams are ladders closed by G, which `gt ladder` draws; the
[Hedin's expansion examples](/docs/examples/hedins-expansion-and-gt) show the whole figure. `arcs` also takes
`boson=` for the style of the arcs (`screened`), `label` for the first arc, `spacing` between the vertices
(0.9 units), `dressed` for a double electron line, `vertex=` and `dir`. The vertices are `v1`, `v2`, ….

## Standing up

`fock`, `gw` and `fan-migdal` take `vertical`. The interaction then runs straight up from the lower vertex `a` to
the upper one `b`, and the electron arcs round on the right, in at the bottom and out at the top. The
interaction carries no arrow, as no boson line does; its label sits on the left, and the electron's outside the
arc on the right.

```feynman source
fock vertical
caption "Fock"
| gw vertical
caption "GW"
| fan-migdal vertical dressed
caption "Fan–Migdal"
```

`legLabel` labels the external electron, under the leg at `a` and over the leg at `b`, and the interaction's
label can say which momentum it carries:

```feynman source
fock vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" G="m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" v="v(\mathbf{q})"
| gw vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" W="W(\mathbf{q})"
```

`mom=` draws that momentum as an arrow beside the interaction instead, and lengthens the interaction to make room
for it; the [momentum guide](/docs/guides/momentum) covers it.

The options of the standing forms:

| Option | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vertical` | off | stands the self-energy up |
| `height` | automatic | the distance from `a` to `b`, in units: 2.2; around an inner diagram, the length of the inner diagram plus 0.9 for each piece of the interaction; longer with `mom=`. The template reference writes the automatic value as 0 |
| `legLabel` | | labels the external electron |
| `inner` | | `chi` or `bubble`: the diagram inside the interaction, from `c` to `d` (`fock` and `fan-migdal`) |
| `chi` | χ or Π | labels the lens `X`; with `inner=bubble`, the lowest piece of the bubble's left line |
| `vtop` | | labels the upper piece of the interaction, from `d` to `b` |
| `ladder` | 0 | the number of rungs across the bubble |
| `rung` | `screened` | the style of the rungs |
| `rungLabel` | | labels the first rung |
| `dir` | `right` | `left`: the electron comes in at `b` and leaves at `a` |

These options are errors where they would do nothing: `height`, `inner`, `chi`, `vtop`, `ladder`, `rung` and
`rungLabel` without `vertical`; `ladder`, `rung` and `rungLabel` without `inner=bubble`; `chi` and `vtop` without
`inner=`. `gw` takes no `inner=`. A `height=` too short for the pieces of the interaction around an inner diagram
is an error that names the shortest height that fits.

## Inside the interaction

Standing up, `fock` and `fan-migdal` take `inner=`, which splits the interaction around a diagram of its own:

- `inner=chi` puts in a response-function lens, `X`, labelled with `chi=`: χ in `fock`, Π in `fan-migdal`.
- `inner=bubble` puts in a polarization bubble of two electron lines, and `ladder=` adds rungs across it.
  `rung=` sets their style (`screened`) and `rungLabel=` labels the first one.

```feynman source
fock vertical inner=chi
caption "inner=chi"
| fock vertical inner=bubble
caption "inner=bubble"
| fock vertical inner=bubble ladder=2 rungLabel=W
caption "ladder=2"
```

The screened interaction is W = v + vχv, with χ the density response function. With χ inside, the Fock diagram
becomes the correlation self-energy iG(W − v), and with the plain Fock diagram it makes GW. The bubble is the
lowest term of χ, the polarization of independent electrons, which makes the second-order direct diagram; the
rungs inside it are the electron–hole attraction that excitons come from.

The inner diagram runs from `c` (below) to `d` (above). They take the vertex kind of `a` and `b` when that is one
of the dots (`dot`, `charge`, `current`, `eph`, `dipole`, `spin`), and are plain dots otherwise. `vtop=` labels
the upper piece of the interaction when it differs from the lower one. In a crystal, for instance, χ joins two
reciprocal lattice vectors G and G′, and the two pieces of v carry q + G and q + G′:

```feynman source
fock vertical inner=chi v="v(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})" vtop="v(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}')" chi="\chi_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}'}(\mathbf{q})"
```

## Naming the points

Every vertex of a template keeps its name, which the **By hand** tab shows, and so does `grid` on a line of its own
([The names of the vertices](/docs/guides/templates#the-names-of-the-vertices)): `a` and `b` where the electron
meets the interaction, `c` and `d` at the ends of the inner diagram, `X` for the lens, and `l1`, `r1`, `l2`,
`r2`, … for the ends of the rungs in a bubble, from the bottom.

```feynman source
grid
fock vertical inner=chi
| fock vertical inner=bubble ladder=1
```

A line that starts with the name of a point labels it afterwards, with `pos=` for where the label goes. Here the
points carry their positions, r and r′ on the electron line and r₁ and r₂ at the ends of χ, so that the
interaction reads v(r, r₁) χ(r₁, r₂) v(r₂, r′), the correlation part of W(r, r′):

```feynman source
fock vertical inner=chi
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
c "\mathbf{r}_1" pos=se
d "\mathbf{r}_2" pos=ne
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=above
```

The same works for every template. Here the Hartree loop is labelled with the density at r′, and the Fock
diagram runs right to left (`dir=left`), so that the electron comes in at r′ and leaves at r:

```feynman source
hartree G="-iG(\mathbf{r}';\mathbf{r}',0^+)" legLabel=n
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=sw
caption "H"
+ fock vertical dir=left G=m legLabel=n
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=above
caption "F"
```

## Bare or dressed lines

`fock` and `hartree` draw the free G⁰. With `dressed` they draw the full G, a double line labelled G; a `G=` you
give is kept. `gw` draws G from the start. `dressed=false` gives it the single line of one-shot G⁰W⁰, with
`G="G^0"` for its label; W is a screened line either way.

```feynman source
fock
caption "first order"
| fock dressed
caption "self-consistent"
| gw dressed=false G="G^0" W="W^0"
caption "G^0W^0"
| gw
caption "GW"
```

`fan-migdal dressed` draws the full G and the full phonon propagator F, a heavy zigzag, and `debye-waller dressed`
draws its loop as F. `sigma2 dressed` dresses every line, and `arcs dressed` only the electron line. Standing up,
`fan-migdal vertical dressed` draws F too, but around an inner diagram the two pieces of the phonon line stay F⁰,
dressed or not: F already holds every insertion of the phonon self-energy Π, and FΠF would count one of them
twice. `F=` and `vtop=` set other labels.

```feynman source
fan-migdal vertical dressed
caption "F"
| fan-migdal vertical dressed inner=chi
caption "F^0ΠF^0"
```

## The direction of the electron

`dir=left` runs the electron the other way, and mirrors `gw gamma=` so that Γ stays where the electron enters: see
[Direction and mirror images](/docs/guides/templates#direction-and-mirror-images).

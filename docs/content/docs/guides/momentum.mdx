An arrowhead marks a fermion, so phonon, photon, field, gluon and interaction lines carry none
([Arrows mark fermions](/docs/guides/notation#arrows-mark-fermions)). The momentum such a line carries goes in its
label, or beside the line as a grey arrow with `mom=`, labelled with the momentum. Here a phonon of momentum q is
absorbed at an electron–phonon vertex and takes the electron from nk to m k+q:

```feynman source
v at (0, 0)
(-1.5, 0) -> v fermion "n\mathbf{k}" above
v -> (1.5, 0) fermion "m\,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" above
(0, -1.3) -- v phonon "\mathbf{q}\nu" side=right mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

The phonon line is written upwards, from its free end to `v`, so its momentum arrow points up, into the vertex.
A line that steep has no above or below: `side=right` counts along the written direction, which upwards is the
page's right, and the momentum arrow takes the other side.

## The interaction of a self-energy

`fock`, `gw` and `fan-migdal` take `mom="\mathbf{q}"`, lying down and standing up. The arrow points towards the
vertex where the electron enters. That is the way q runs when the inner electron carries k+q, as in
Σ(k) = i Σ_q v(q) G(k+q): at that vertex the electron brings k and the interaction brings q, and k+q goes on along
the inner line. `momdir=back` turns the arrow round, for an inner line that carries k−q.

```feynman source
fock legLabel="\mathbf{k}" G="\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" mom="\mathbf{q}"
caption "inner line \mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}"
| fock legLabel="\mathbf{k}" G="\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}" mom="\mathbf{q}" momdir=back
caption "inner line \mathbf{k}−\mathbf{q}: momdir=back"
| fock vertical inner=chi mom="\mathbf{Q}" momtop="\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}'_\parallel" momside=out
caption "momtop=, momside=out"
```

With `dir=left` the electron enters at `b`, and the arrow points there. With `gw gamma=` it points to Γ, where
the electron enters, mirrored or not. Around an inner diagram both pieces of the interaction carry the arrow, and
`momtop=` labels the upper one when its momentum differs, as `vtop=` does the interaction's label: in the third
diagram the two pieces carry Q and Q + G′∥, which differ by a reciprocal lattice vector.

Standing up on a single line, the arrow points down to `a`, where the electron comes in with nk and goes on as
m, k+q:

```feynman source
fock vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" G="m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" v="v(\mathbf{q})" mom="\mathbf{q}"
| gw vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

On these templates `momdir=` takes only `back`, and `momside=` only `in` or `out`. `momdir`, `momside` and
`momtop` without `mom=` are errors, and so is `momtop` without `vertical` and `inner=`; `gw` has no `momtop`.

## Where the arrow sits

The arrow sits inside, between the interaction and the electron, on the other side of the interaction from its
label. A short momentum such as q or Q fits there; `momside=in` says the same as leaving it out. A longer one, such
as Q + G′∥, does not, and `momside=out` puts the arrow outside. The interaction's label makes way:

- lying down, it moves inside the arc;
- standing up, on a single line, it stays outside, low on the line, with the arrow above it;
- around an inner diagram, each label sits near `a` or `b`, and each arrow nearer the middle, as in the third
  diagram above.

```feynman source
fock mom="\mathbf{q}"
caption "inside"
| fock mom="\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}'_\parallel" momside=out
caption "momside=out"
| fock vertical mom="\mathbf{q}"
caption "inside"
| fock vertical mom="\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}'_\parallel" momside=out
caption "momside=out"
```

## Room for the arrow standing up

Standing up, the electron passes close to the interaction near `a` and `b`. So that the arrow and its label clear
it, `mom=` lengthens the interaction: a single line to 2.8 units, and each piece around an inner diagram to 1.45,
or to 1.6 with `momside=out`, where the label and the arrow sit one above the other. A `height=` you give is kept
as long as it leaves the arrow room. One shorter than 2.6, or than 1.35 a piece around an inner diagram, is an
error that names the shortest height that fits.

| Length in units, standing up | Without `mom=` | With `mom=` | With `momside=out` | Shortest `height=` with `mom=` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The interaction, one line | 2.2 | 2.8 | 2.8 | 2.6 |
| Each piece around an inner diagram | 0.9 | 1.45 | 1.6 | 1.35 a piece |
| All of `fock vertical inner=chi` | 3.2 | 4.3 | 4.6 | 4.1 |

These are the lengths at 40 px per unit and more. The arrow and its label keep their size in px, so below 40 px
per unit they take more units: with `mom=`, the lengths grow by 40/unit, and so do the inner diagram and, lying
down, the default width of 2.8 units. The legs do not, and nothing grows without `mom=`. The shortest `height=`
accepted grows likewise, and a `height=` or `width=` that the block gives is kept as written. The unit can come
from the block (`unit 30`), from the fence (`unit=30`) or from the site
([Site-wide notation and unit](/docs/installation#site-wide-notation-and-unit)). This block is drawn with `unit=30`
in its fence, so the interaction with the arrow stands 2.8 × 40/30 ≈ 3.73 units tall, as its **By hand** tab shows,
and the one without stays at 2.2:

```feynman source unit=30
fock vertical mom="\mathbf{q}"
| fock vertical
```

## Written out by hand

In the **By hand** tab the interaction is an ordinary line with `mom=`, written from `a` to `b`: from the tip of Γ
with `gamma=`, upwards when standing, and in two pieces around an inner diagram, `a` to `c` and `d` to `b`. A
line's momentum arrow runs along it, from its first end to its second, unless `momdir=back`. So the template
options come out as:

| Template | By hand |
| --- | --- |
| an arrow towards `a`, or Γ | `momdir=back` |
| an arrow towards `b` | no `momdir` |
| `momside=out` | `momside=left`; `momside=right` for `gw gamma= dir=left`, the mirror image |
| `momside=out`, lying down | `side=in` on a labelled line, which puts the label inside |
| `momside=out`, standing up | `pos=` for the label and `mompos=` for the arrow, on a labelled line |
| `momtop=` | the `mom=` of the upper piece, from `d` to `b` |

```feynman source
gw gamma="\Gamma" mom="\mathbf{q}" momside=out
| gw gamma="\Gamma" dir=left mom="\mathbf{q}" momside=out
```

## Lines you write

Any line takes `mom=`, a fermion line too, and `momdir=`, `momside=` and `mompos=` place its arrow (the
[language reference](/docs/reference/language) lists every line option):

- The arrow runs along the written direction, from the line's first end to its second, whatever arrowhead the line
  carries: `a <- b fermion mom="k"` has its momentum arrow from a to b. `momdir=back` turns it round.
- On a straight line or an arc it sits at the label's place along the line, on the other side of the line, or
  opposite where a label would go when there is none. On a straight line with no label, `above` or `below` puts
  the arrow on that side of the page. `momside=left` or `momside=right` puts it on that side of the written
  direction; on the label's side, move one of the two along the line with `pos=` or `mompos=`. Loops come below.
- `mompos=` moves it along the line, as a fraction from 0 to 1. It is about 30 px long where the line has room,
  and keeps clear of the line's ends and of the line's own arrowhead.
- A leg is written left to right, or bottom to top when it points straight up or down, so its momentum arrow runs
  that way unless `momdir=back`.
- `momdir=`, `momside=` or `mompos=` on a line or a leg without `mom=` is an error, as there is no arrow to place.

Two electrons exchange the momentum q through v. The Coulomb line is written from `b` up to `a`, so its arrow
points up and carries q from the lower electron to the upper one; its label takes the left of the written
direction, and the arrow the right:

```feynman source
a at (0, 0.8); b at (0, -0.8)
(-1.5, 0.8) -> a fermion "\mathbf{k}" above
a -> (1.5, 0.8) fermion "\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" above
(-1.5, -0.8) -> b fermion "\mathbf{k}'" below
b -> (1.5, -0.8) fermion "\mathbf{k}'-\mathbf{q}" below
b -- a coulomb "v(\mathbf{q})" mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

On a loop the arrow goes outside the loop, on its far half, away from the vertex and the lines there: halfway
round when the label is inside or there is none, and a sixth of the way round from a label outside. Here the
phonon of the Debye–Waller diagram carries q round its loop:

```feynman source
v at (0, 0) hatched "g^{DW}" pos=below
leg v left 0.7; leg v right 0.7
v -- v phonon loop above size=1.2 "F^0" mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

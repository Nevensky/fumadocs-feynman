These templates draw the diagrams in which two or more particles propagate together: the polarization and its RPA
series, excitons and the ladders of the Bethe–Salpeter equation, four-point vertices and their equations, vertex
corrections, ladders of three and four particles, and the GT self-energy. Electrons are G (G⁰ when free), and a
double line is a dressed propagator; W is the screened interaction, v the bare Coulomb one, D the photon and F the
phonon. [Using templates](/docs/guides/templates) explains how to write options and how to add statements of your
own, and the [template reference](/docs/reference/templates) lists every option with its default.

## Polarization bubble

The polarization P describes the electron–hole pairs that a perturbation creates. In the bubble, two electron
lines join its two vertices, one running forward (the electron) and one back (the hole). Without vertex corrections
it is P = −iGG, the bubble of the random-phase approximation (RPA).

```feynman source
bubble vertex=charge ext=coulomb extLabel=v
| bubble vertex=current ext=photon extLabel=D
```

- `ext=` sets the external lines: `coulomb` (the default), `photon`, `phonon`, `screened`, or `none` to leave
  them out. `extLabel=` labels the one on the left.
- `vertex=` sets both vertices, and `left=` and `right=` one of them: `charge` for the density ρ, which couples to
  a Coulomb line, `current` for the current j, which couples to a photon, `eph`, `dipole` and `spin` for the other
  couplings, and `disk` for a screened vertex. [Notation](/docs/guides/notation#matrix-elements-on-vertices) labels
  `a0` and `b0` with the matrix elements ρ and j.
- `top=` and `bottom=` label the two electron lines, `dressed` draws them as G, and `label=` writes a name inside
  the bubble (not with `ladder=` or `gamma=`).
- `width=` sets the width of the bubble (1.6 units); with `ladder=`, the bubble is at least as wide as its rungs
  need.
- `ladder=` puts rungs inside, `rung=` and `rungLabel=` set their style and label (`screened`, W); `exciton`
  below is this ladder with other defaults.

With phonon lines, the bubble is the phonon self-energy Π. One of its vertices is bare and the other screened (a
disk): with the bubble between them, screening both would count the screening twice.

```feynman source
bubble ext=phonon left=dot right=disk dressed top=G bottom=G
```

`gamma=` closes the bubble with Hedin's vertex Γ instead of a bare vertex, as in Hedin's equations,
P = −iGGΓ; Γ = 1 gives back the RPA bubble. `gcolor=` sets the colour of the triangle (`c4`, teal).

```feynman source
bubble gamma="\Gamma" top=G bottom=G dressed ext=screened
```

## RPA screening

The RPA sums the bubbles into the screened interaction, W = v + vPv + vPvPv + … = v + vPW. `rpa` draws a chain of
`n=` bubbles (3) joined by `link=` lines (`coulomb`); `linkLabel=` (v) labels the first link. The external lines
take the style of the links unless `ext=` sets another, and `extLabel=` labels the first. Most options of `bubble`
(`vertex`, `dressed`, `ladder`, …) apply to every bubble, and its labels (`top`, `bottom`, `rungLabel`) to the
first. As terms of an equation, the chains draw the series:

```feynman source
propagator style=coulomb dressed label=W
= propagator style=coulomb label=v
+ rpa n=1 extLabel=v
+ rpa n=2 extLabel=v
+ ...
```

The [Dyson equations](/docs/examples/dyson-equations) examples show this series next to the Dyson equations for G,
F and D.

## Excitons

An exciton is an electron and a hole bound by their screened attraction. In the polarization it shows as a ladder
of W between the two lines of the bubble: the Bethe–Salpeter equation sums the ladder, and its bound states are the
excitons. `exciton` draws the bubble with `n=` rungs (3). Its vertices are current vertices and its external lines
photons D, as in optical absorption. The flag `charge` sets charge vertices and screened external lines
(`vertex=charge ext=screened extLabel=W`), and the flag `current` sets the defaults back.

```feynman source
exciton n=3
caption "current"
| exciton n=3 charge
caption "charge"
```

The kernel of the Bethe–Salpeter equation also has an exchange part v̄, the Coulomb interaction without its
long-range part, in which the pair annihilates and a new one is created. It joins ladder bubbles into a chain:
`bubbles=` sets how many, and `link=` and `linkLabel=` the line between them (without them, the links repeat the
external line).

```feynman source
exciton charge n=2 bubbles=2 link=coulomb linkLabel="\bar{v}" ext=coulomb extLabel=v
```

## Ladders

`ladder` draws two lines that exchange `n=` interactions (3).

- In the particle–hole channel (`ph`, the default) the upper line runs right and the lower one left: the
  electron–hole ladder of the Bethe–Salpeter equation.
- In the particle–particle channel (`pp`) both run right: two particles that scatter again and again, as in the
  T matrix. With phonon rungs this is the Cooper channel of superconductivity.

```feynman source
ladder n=4 top=G bottom=G
| ladder n=3 pp rung=phonon rungLabel=F
```

`rung=` and `rungLabel=` set the rungs (`screened`, W), `top=` and `bottom=` label the lines, `h=` is the distance
between the lines and `spacing=` the distance between rungs, in units.

`crossed` draws the maximally crossed ladder, in which the first vertex of the upper line is joined to the last of
the lower one, and so on. With impurity lines between a retarded electron G<sup>R</sup> and an advanced one
G<sup>A</sup>, its sum is the Cooperon, which gives the weak-localization correction to the conductivity.
`ladder crossed` crosses the rungs of any ladder.

```feynman source
crossed n=4
```

## Four-point vertices

`box` draws a four-point vertex with a leg on each corner. `label=` names it (Γ), `color=` sets its colour, and the
flags `ph` and `pp` set the way the lower legs run.

```feynman source
box
| box pp
```

`bse` draws the Bethe–Salpeter equation for the two-particle correlation function, L = L⁰ + L⁰KL: L is two
independent propagators, L⁰, plus L⁰ followed by the kernel K and by L again. For excitons in the GW approximation,
K holds the exchange v̄ and the screened direct interaction W. `L=` and `K=` set the labels, and `pp` the channel.

```feynman source
bse
```

`tmatrix` draws the T-matrix equation, T = V + VGGT: the ladder of a bare interaction V (`interaction=coulomb`)
summed in the particle–particle channel (`pp`, the default; `ph` for the other). `G=` labels the two propagators of
the last term.

```feynman source
tmatrix
```

`parquet` draws the parquet decomposition of the full vertex F. Each diagram of F is either fully irreducible, Λ,
or reducible in exactly one of the three two-particle channels: particle–hole (Φ<sub>ph</sub>), transverse
particle–hole (Φ<sub>p̄h̄</sub>) and particle–particle (Φ<sub>pp</sub>). Each reducible part is a vertex irreducible
in its channel, joined to F by two lines in that channel. `captions=false` leaves out the captions.

```feynman source
parquet
```

The [two-particle examples](/docs/examples/two-particle) collect these.

## Vertex corrections

`vertex-correction` draws a three-point vertex: the external line arrives at `v`, an electron comes in on the
lower line and leaves on the upper one, and `n=` interactions (1) run across the two electron lines. On the left,
the electron–photon vertex corrected by a phonon; on the right, the Coulomb vertex corrected by W.

```feynman source
vertex-correction ext=photon rung=phonon rungLabel=F
| vertex-correction ext=coulomb rung=screened rungLabel=W
```

Migdal's theorem makes the phonon correction small, of order √(m/M), when the momentum transfer is large
(v<sub>F</sub>q ≫ ω). A photon brings q ≈ 0, where the theorem does not hold.

With `n=2` and more, the interactions form a ladder across the vertex, and the flag `crossed` crosses them (the
flag `ladder` is the default):

```feynman source
vertex-correction n=2 ext=screened rung=screened rungLabel=W
caption "ladder"
| vertex-correction n=2 crossed ext=screened rung=screened
caption "crossed"
```

`hedin-vertex` draws Hedin's equation for the vertex function, Γ = 1 + (δΣ/δG) GGΓ: a Bethe–Salpeter equation with
the kernel δΣ/δG. With Σ = iGW and the change of W neglected, the kernel is iW, and the first iteration gives the
first-order correction above. `Gamma=` and `kernel=` set the labels, `ext=` the line at the tip of the triangle
(`screened`), and `gcolor=` and `color=` the colours of the triangle and of the kernel's box.

```feynman source
hedin-vertex
```

The vertex enters the self-energy as Σ = iGWΓ (`gw gamma=`, in [Self-energies](/docs/guides/self-energies)) and the
polarization as P = −iGGΓ (`bubble gamma=`, above). More in [Vertex corrections](/docs/examples/vertex-corrections).

## Trions, biexcitons and other ladders

`nbody` draws a ladder of any number of particle lines, with rungs between any two of them.

- `lines=` lists the particles from the top: `e` for an electron, whose arrow runs along `dir`, and `h` for a hole,
  whose arrow runs against it.
- `rungs=` lists the columns from left to right, each a pair of line numbers counted from the top. `12` joins lines
  1 and 2. `13` joins lines 1 and 3 and passes over line 2, which shows a gap. `12+34` puts two rungs side by side
  in one column; rungs in one column must not overlap.
- `labels=auto` names the lines `e` and `h`, numbered when a kind repeats (`e_1`, `e_2`); `labels=none` leaves the
  names out, and `labels="..."` gives your own, separated by spaces.
- `rung=` and `rungLabel=` set the rungs (`screened`, W), `gap=` the distance between lines and `spacing=` the
  distance between columns.

The arrows run with the electrons and against the holes, so one W serves for every pair: it binds an electron to a
hole and repels two electrons or two holes. `trion` and `biexciton` are ready-made ladders of three and four lines:

```feynman source
nbody
caption "exciton"
| trion
caption "trion X^{−}"
| biexciton
caption "biexciton"
```

`trion` is two electrons and a hole (the flag `negative`, X⁻, the default) or two holes and an electron (the flag
`positive`, X⁺); its rungs, `12 23 13 12 23`, join every pair of lines. In `biexciton`, `12+34 13 24 23 14 12+34`,
the rungs 12 and 34 bind each exciton and the others join the two excitons. These are direct terms: the exchange
diagrams, in which the ends of two identical particles are swapped, are not drawn.

Your own ladders take the same syntax. Here an exciton (lines 1 and 2) meets an electron (line 3), which attracts
its hole (23) and repels its electron (13), and goes on:

```feynman source
nbody lines="e h e" rungs="12 12 23 13 12 12"
```

More in [Trions and biexcitons](/docs/examples/trions-and-biexcitons).

## GT

The GT self-energy of Müller, Blügel and Friedrich, PRB 100, 045130 (2019), closes a T matrix with an electron
line: Σ<sup>σ</sup>(13) = −i T<sup>σσ′</sup>(12,34) G<sup>σ′</sup>(24). T is the ladder of W between the electron
(spin σ) and a line of the opposite spin σ′, summed to all orders. Where W carries plasmons, this T matrix carries
magnons, the spin waves and Stoner excitations. `gt` draws it the way GW is drawn (their Fig. 2), with T bent into
an arch:

```feynman source
gw dressed=false G="\uparrow" dir=left
| gt n=5 label="GT" dir=left
```

The outer rail continues the external electron (↑); the inner rail and the line that closes the loop along the base
carry ↓. `n=` sets the radial rungs (3), besides the two along the base line, and `label=` writes a name inside the
arch. `spin=false` leaves out the spin labels, and `up=` and `down=` change them.

The flags `box` and `ladder` draw the same self-energy as a T box closed by G, after `n=` rungs (0), or as a closed
ladder of `n=` rungs (3, and 2 at least). `pp` uses a particle–particle T matrix: its rails run the same way, and
the line that closes it runs against both. It carries no magnon.

```feynman source
gt box
| gt ladder
| gt pp
```

In the box and ladder forms, `numbers` numbers the points as in the paper's Fig. 3, `T=` and `color=` set the
box's label and colour, and the external electron lies on the main line, so that `gt` lines up with `gw` in a row;
`center` puts the middle of the ladder there instead. `legs=false` leaves out the external legs, and `dir=left`
runs the electron from right to left, as in the paper.

`gt-bse` writes the T matrix in the ladder approximation, each term closed by G: T starts at third order in W,
T = WKWKW + WKT, with K(12,34) = iG(13)G(42) the free electron–hole pair (their Fig. 3). By default it numbers the
points and leaves out the spins and the legs.

```feynman source
gt-bse dir=left
```

<Callout type="info" title="G(24), not G(42)">
The paper's Eq. 1 prints G(42). With its K(12,34) = iG(13)G(42), the line inside T already runs from 2 to 4, so the
loop closes with G(24), from 4 back to 2, as its Fig. 3 draws. The templates draw G(24).
</Callout>

[Hedin's expansion and GT](/docs/examples/hedins-expansion-and-gt) places GT among the third-order diagrams of
Hedin's expansion.

fumadocs-feynman is made for many-body physics, but its lines are those of particle physics too: fermions, photons,
gluons, scalars and ghosts. These templates draw the standard diagrams of particle physics, in the same `feynman`
code blocks. [Using templates](/docs/guides/templates) explains how to write options, and the
[template reference](/docs/reference/templates) lists every option with its default.

The conventions are the usual ones:

- **Time runs left to right.** Incoming particles are on the left, outgoing ones on the right.
- **Arrows mark fermions.** A fermion's arrow follows the particle: towards the vertex as it comes in, away from it
  as it leaves. An antiparticle's arrow runs the other way, back in time. Ghosts carry arrows too. Photon, gluon,
  scalar and other boson lines carry none.
- **Labels are particle names.** Every leg and propagator takes a label in TeX, such as `e^-`, `\mu^+`, `\gamma`,
  `g`, `Z^0` or `H`.

Fermions are drawn in the electron colour, photons rose, gluons violet and scalars in the text colour.
[Notation](/docs/guides/notation) lists every line style.

## The s, t and u channels

`s-channel` draws two particles that annihilate into one propagator, which then makes the outgoing pair. By default
this is e⁻e⁺ → μ⁻μ⁺ through a photon. `prop=` sets the style of the propagator and `label=` its label.

```feynman source
s-channel
| s-channel prop=boson label="Z^0"
| s-channel prop=scalar label=H mom=q
```

- `in1=` and `in2=` label the incoming lines, upper and lower. `out1=` and `out2=` label the outgoing ones.
- `in=` and `out=` set the style of the incoming and outgoing lines: `fermion` (the default), `photon`, `gluon` or
  `scalar`, for example. On fermion lines, the upper one is the particle and the lower one the antiparticle.
- `mom=` puts a momentum arrow beside the propagator, as on any line ([Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum)).

`t-channel` draws two particles that exchange a line, one above the other: e⁻μ⁻ → e⁻μ⁻ by default. `anti` makes the
lower line an antiparticle, with its arrows running right to left. `u-channel` swaps the two outgoing particles,
which only matters when they are identical. It is drawn with the outgoing lines crossing, one over the other.

```feynman source
t-channel
| t-channel anti in2="e^+" out2="e^+"
| u-channel
```

They take the same options as `s-channel`, and `height=` sets the distance between their two vertices.

### In equations

A line that starts with `+`, `-` or `=` adds a diagram to an equation ([Using templates](/docs/guides/templates#templates-in-equations)).
Møller scattering is the t channel minus the u channel, and Bhabha scattering the t channel minus the s channel:

```feynman source
t-channel in2="e^-" out2="e^-"
- u-channel
```

```feynman source
t-channel anti in2="e^+" out2="e^+"
- s-channel out1="e^-" out2="e^+"
```

## Compton scattering and pair annihilation

`compton` draws e⁻γ → e⁻γ along a straight electron line: the electron absorbs the photon and then emits one. With
`crossed`, it emits first, and the two photons cross over the line.

```feynman source
compton
+ compton crossed
```

`annihilation` draws e⁻e⁺ → γγ. The electron line runs from the incoming electron to the incoming positron and gives
off a photon at each vertex. `crossed` swaps the photons.

```feynman source
annihilation
+ annihilation crossed
```

Both take `line=` and `boson=` for the styles of the fermion and the boson lines, `in1=`, `in2=`, `out1=` and `out2=`
for the labels of the legs, and `label=` for the internal line. With `boson=gluon` and quark labels, they draw the
same processes in QCD.

## QED and QCD loops

The one-loop diagrams of QED come from the many-body templates, with a photon in place of the phonon or the Coulomb
line:

```feynman source
fan-migdal phonon=photon G="e^-" F="\gamma"
| bubble ext=photon top="e^-" bottom="e^+"
| vertex-correction ext=photon rung=photon
```

- [`fan-migdal`](/docs/reference/templates#fan-migdal) with `phonon=photon` is the electron self-energy.
- [`bubble`](/docs/reference/templates#bubble) with `ext=photon` is the vacuum polarisation.
- [`vertex-correction`](/docs/reference/templates#vertex-correction) with `ext=photon rung=photon` is the QED vertex
  correction.

In QCD, `bubble ext=gluon` gives the gluon self-energy. `line=` sets what runs round the loop: gluons, ghosts or quarks.

```feynman source
bubble ext=gluon line=gluon
+ bubble ext=gluon line=ghost
+ bubble ext=gluon line=fermion top=q bottom="\bar{q}"
```

## Triangle and box loops

`triangle-loop` draws one line into a loop of three propagators and two lines out. By default it is the Higgs boson
decaying to two photons through a loop of top quarks. `fusion` turns it round, so the two lines come in on the left
and the single line leaves on the right, as in Higgs production by gluon fusion.

```feynman source
triangle-loop
| triangle-loop fusion legs=gluon top=g bottom=g
```

- `ext=` and `label=` set the style and label of the single line (`scalar`, `H`).
- `legs=`, `top=` and `bottom=` set the style and labels of the pair (`photon`, `\gamma`).
- `loop=` and `loopLabel=` set the style and label of the loop (`fermion`, `t`).

`box-loop` puts four lines on a loop of four propagators. By default it draws light-by-light scattering, γγ → γγ.
It takes `legs=`, `loop=`, `loopLabel=` and `in1=`, `in2=`, `out1=`, `out2=` for the four legs.

```feynman source
box-loop loopLabel="e^-"
```

## Other diagrams

Where no template fits, write the diagram by hand: place the vertices, then draw the lines between them. A line
written `->` carries an arrow from its first end to its second, and `--` carries none:

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (2, 0)
(-1, 0.9) -> a fermion "e^-" above
a -> (-1, -0.9) fermion "e^+" below
a -- b photon "\gamma" above
b -> (3, 0.9) fermion "\mu^-" above
(3, -0.9) -> b fermion "\mu^+" below
```

The **By hand** tab under every template above shows that template written out this way, ready to copy and change.
[Writing diagrams](/docs/guides/writing-diagrams) covers the language, and
[Particle physics](/docs/examples/particle-physics) has every example of this page.

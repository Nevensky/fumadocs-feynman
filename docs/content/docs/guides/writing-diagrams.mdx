title: Writing diagrams
description: A tour of the feynman language, from vertices and lines to labels and equations.
---

A diagram is a `feynman` code block in an MDX page. Each line of the block is a statement: place a vertex,
draw a line between two vertices, add an external leg. This block draws the Fan–Migdal self-energy:

````md
```feynman
a at (0, 0) disk; b at (2.8, 0) disk
leg a left; leg b right
b -> a fermion dressed "G" below
a -- b phonon dressed bulge=1.2 above "F"
```
````

On the page it becomes:

```feynman
a at (0, 0) disk; b at (2.8, 0) disk
leg a left; leg b right
b -> a fermion dressed "G" below
a -- b phonon dressed bulge=1.2 above "F"
```

This page goes through the statements one at a time. Every diagram below is followed by its source; where
one uses a template, the **By hand** tab shows the same diagram written out statement by statement
([Source tabs](/docs/guides/source-tabs)). The [language reference](/docs/reference/language) lists every
statement and option in tables.

## Statements and comments

Write one statement per line, or several on one line separated by `;`. A `%` or `//` starts a comment that
runs to the end of the line. Inside a quoted label, `;`, `%` and `//` are text. Spaces at the start of a line
are ignored, so you can indent freely.

```feynman source
% Fock exchange, by hand
a at (0, 0); b at (2.4, 0)    // the two vertices
leg a left; leg b right       // the external electron
a -> b fermion "G^0" below
a -- b coulomb arc above "v"  % the Coulomb line, a half circle
```

Most statements draw something: a vertex (`a at (0, 0)`), a line (`a -> b fermion`), a leg (`leg a left`),
text, or a whole diagram from a [template](/docs/guides/templates) (`fock`). Three set up the whole block,
wherever they stand: `unit`, `grid` and `style`.

## Coordinates and units

Place a vertex with `at` and a position `(x, y)`. x runs to the right and y up, in units of 40 px. The
brackets are optional: `a at 2.4,0` is the same vertex as `a at (2.4, 0)`.

Put the main line of a diagram on y = 0. In an equation, the operators (`=`, `+`) are set at that height, so
diagrams whose main lines lie on y = 0 line up with them and with each other.

A line can also end at a bare position in brackets: `(-1.5, 0) -> v` runs from the point (−1.5, 0) to the
vertex `v`, and nothing is drawn at the point ([Points without names](#points-without-names)).

While you place things, add a line that says `grid`. It draws the unit grid under the diagram, marks y = 0,
and writes the name of each vertex beside it. Delete the line when the diagram is done.

```feynman source
grid
v at (0, 0)
(-2, 0) -> v fermion "n\mathbf{k}" above
v -> (2, 0) fermion "m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" above
(0, -1.3) -- v phonon "\mathbf{q}\nu"
```

`unit 30` sets the size of a unit to 30 px for the block it stands in. Only the geometry scales: strokes,
arrowheads and labels keep their size, so a smaller unit gives a tighter diagram with the same type. Here is
the Fan–Migdal block from the top of the page at 30 px:

```feynman source
unit 30
a at (0, 0) disk; b at (2.8, 0) disk
leg a left; leg b right
b -> a fermion dressed "G" below
a -- b phonon dressed bulge=1.2 above "F"
```

The fence can set the unit too, with `unit=30` in its meta
([Remark plugin](/docs/reference/remark-plugin#fence-meta)), and so can the whole site
([Site-wide notation and unit](/docs/installation#site-wide-notation-and-unit)); a `unit` statement in the block
wins over both.

## Vertices

A vertex is a name, `at` and a position, then optionally a shape, options and a label in double quotes:
`NAME at x,y [shape] [options] ["label"]`. A name starts with a letter or `_` and may hold letters (Greek
ones too), digits, `_`, primes (`b'`) and a `-` between two parts (`a-1`). It cannot hold a `.`, which marks an
[anchor](#anchors).

Without a shape, a vertex is a small dot. The shapes are:

- small dots for couplings: `dot`, `charge` (in ink, for ρ), `current` (teal, for j), `eph` (amber, the
  electron–phonon g), `dipole` (rose, d·E) and `spin` (violet, σ·B);
- marks: `disk` (a screened vertex), `hatched`, `open`, `cross`, `otimes` and `square`;
- shapes that hold their label: `blob` (a self-energy), `bubble` (blue), `box` (a four-point vertex),
  `triangle` and `lens` (a response function);
- `none`, which draws nothing.

Lines stop at the edge of the larger shapes by themselves. [Vocabulary](/docs/examples/vocabulary#vertex-shapes)
shows every shape.

```feynman source
g at (0, 0) eph "g"
j at (1.2, 0) current "j"
r at (2.4, 0) charge "\rho"
x at (3.6, 0) otimes "V"
S at (0.2, -1.6) blob "\Sigma"
P at (1.6, -1.6) bubble "P"
K at (3, -1.6) box "K"
X at (4.4, -1.6) lens "\chi"
```

A label goes below a small vertex and inside a blob, bubble, box, triangle or lens. `pos=` puts it
elsewhere: `above`, `below`, `left`, `right`, `ne`, `nw`, `se`, `sw`, or an angle in degrees, anticlockwise
from the right (`pos=30`). On the shapes that hold their label, any `pos=` but `center` puts it outside.

```feynman source
x at (0, 1.2) otimes "V_{imp}" pos=above
v at (0, 0)
(-1.6, 0) -> v fermion "\mathbf{k}" below
v -> (1.6, 0) fermion "\mathbf{k}'" below
x -- v coulomb "\mathbf{q}"
```

The other options:

- `color=` sets the colour of the shape ([Notation](/docs/guides/notation#colours) lists the colour names).
- `r=` sets the radius in units of any shape but a box or lens.
- `w=` and `h=` set the width and height in units of a box or lens.
- `dir=` turns a triangle (it points right by default) or lays a lens down (`dir=right`).
- `size=` sets the size in px of a label outside the shape.
- `faint` fades the vertex.

To label or restyle a vertex that already exists, a template's too, write its name followed by a label, a
shape or options. It stays where it is. The **By hand** tab shows the names of a template's vertices.

```feynman source
fock
a current "\mathbf{r}" pos=sw
b current "\mathbf{r}'" pos=se
```

Writing `a at x,y` again places a new vertex `a` instead, shape and label included.

## Lines

A line joins two ends with an operator: `->` draws an arrow from the first end to the second, `<-` from the
second to the first, and `--` none. After the ends come, in any order, a line style, options and flags, and
a label in double quotes.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2.4, 0) fermion "e"
(0, -0.9) <- (2.4, -0.9) fermion "h"
(0, -1.8) -- (2.4, -1.8) phonon "F"
```

Without a style, `->` and `<-` draw a `fermion` and `--` a `plain` line. The styles are `fermion`, `phonon`
(zigzag), `photon` (wave), `coulomb` (dashed), `screened` (double dashed), `field` (a wave on dashes),
`gluon`, `scalar`, `ghost`, `plain`, `boson` and `impurity` (dashed, with a cross).
[Line styles](/docs/examples/vocabulary#line-styles) shows them all.

<Callout type="info">
Arrows mark fermions: an electron, or a hole running back. A `phonon`, `photon`, `field`, `gluon`, `coulomb`,
`screened` or `impurity` line written with `->` or `<-` is an error. Draw it with `--`, and put the momentum it
carries in its label or in a `mom=` arrow beside it ([Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum)).
</Callout>

### Labels on lines

A label sits halfway along the line, on the left of the direction the line is written in: above a line
written left to right, below one written right to left. `above` and `below` name a side of the page instead,
whatever order the ends are written in:

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2, 0) fermion "G"        % written left to right: above
(5, 0) -> (3, 0) fermion "G"        % written right to left: below
(8, 0) -> (6, 0) fermion "G" above  % a side of the page, in either order
```

`above` and `below` work on lines within 45° of horizontal. On a steeper line, write `side=left` or
`side=right`: it counts along the written direction, so on a line written upwards, `right` is the page's
right.

```feynman source
v at (0, 0)
(-1.4, 0) -> v fermion "\mathbf{k}" below
v -> (1.4, 0) fermion "\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" below
v -- (0, 1.3) photon "\mathbf{q}" side=right
```

`pos=0.3` moves a label along the line (0 at the first end, 1 at the second), and `dist=12` sets its distance
from the line in px.

### Curves

`bulge=1.2 above` bends a line into an arc that rises 1.2 units above the straight line between its ends;
`below` bends it the other way. The height is in the same units as the positions, so the arc keeps its height
when you move a vertex. `arc above` is a half circle, and `bend above` a flatter arc, 0.35 times as high as
the ends are apart.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (2.8, 0)
a -> b fermion
a -- b phonon bulge=0.5 above   % 0.5 units high
a -- b photon bend above        % 0.35 × 2.8 ≈ 1 unit high
a -- b coulomb arc above        % a half circle, 1.4 units high
```

A bulge may be at most half the distance between the ends; for more than a half circle, use `bend=`.
`bend=0.42` gives the arc's height as a fraction of the distance between the ends (0.5 is a half circle).
Without `above` or `below`, its sign counts from the written direction: a positive `bend` bulges to the left
of the line from its first end to its second.

The label of an arc goes outside it; `side=in` puts it inside. The arrow never changes the shape; the order of
the ends does. `a -> b` and `a <- b` draw the same arc with the arrow turned round, while `b -> a` bends to the
other side unless `above` or `below` gives the side.

### How a line looks

`dressed` draws the full propagator: `fermion`, `scalar` and `plain` become double lines, `coulomb` becomes
`screened`, and `phonon`, `photon`, `field`, `gluon` and `boson` are drawn heavy; `screened`, `ghost` and
`impurity` have no dressed form. `bare` turns a dressed style back.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2, 0) fermion "G^0"
(3, 0) -> (5, 0) fermion dressed "G"
(0, -1) -- (2, -1) phonon "F^0"
(3, -1) -- (5, -1) phonon dressed "F"
(0, -2) -- (2, -2) coulomb "v"
(3, -2) -- (5, -2) coulomb dressed "W"
```

`heavy` and `thin` set the stroke, `double` draws two strands, `color=` sets the colour of the line, its
arrow and its label, and `faint` fades the line. `width=`, `amp=`, `wl=` and `dash=` set the stroke width,
the amplitude and wavelength of a wave or zigzag, and the dash pattern of a dashed line, all in px.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2, 0) fermion color=violet "G_\uparrow"
(3, 0) -> (5, 0) fermion faint "G_\downarrow"
(0, -1) -- (2, -1) photon amp=5 wl=14 "D"
(3, -1) -- (5, -1) coulomb dash="2 3" "v"
```

### Arrowheads and marks

An arrowhead sits halfway between the two vertices. `arrowpos=0.25` moves it along the line, as a fraction
from the first end; `arrow=end` puts it at the end it points to, `arrow=start` just inside the end it leaves,
and `arrow=none` leaves it out. `marker=cross` draws a cross in the middle of the line, as an `impurity` line
has by default; `marker=none` takes it away.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2.4, 0) fermion "G" arrowpos=0.25
(0, -0.9) -> (2.4, -0.9) fermion "G" arrow=end
(0, -1.8) -> (2.4, -1.8) fermion "G" arrow=start
(0, -2.7) -- (2.4, -2.7) impurity "u"
```

### Momentum

`mom="\mathbf{q}"` draws the momentum a line carries as a small grey arrow beside it, labelled with the
momentum. The arrow runs along the written direction, from the first end to the second, and goes on the side
opposite the line's label. `momdir=back` turns it round, `momside=left` or `momside=right` puts it on that
side of the written direction, and `mompos=0.7` moves it along the line.

```feynman source
v at (0, 0)
(-1.6, 0) -> v fermion "\mathbf{k}" below
v -> (1.6, 0) fermion "\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" below
(0, 1.4) -- v photon "D" mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

[Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum) covers it in full, with the templates that take `mom=`.

## Chains

Write several ends in a row to draw a line between each pair: `a -> b -> c fermion` is the two lines
`a -> b` and `b -> c`. The style, options and flags apply to every piece, and each operator sets the arrow of
its own piece. A chain takes no label: write a labelled line on its own.

```feynman source
v0 at (0, 0); v1 at (1.15, 0); v2 at (2.3, 0); v3 at (3.45, 0)
(-0.8, 0) -> v0 -> v1 -> v2 -> v3 -> (4.25, 0) fermion
v0 -- v3 phonon bulge=1.4 above
v1 -- v2 phonon arc above
```

## Lines that cross

Where two wavy, zigzag or coiled lines cross, the one written later passes over the other, with a short gap in
the line underneath. Mark any other line `over` to make it pass over every line written before it that it
crosses, and write it after them. A dashed line underneath keeps its own gaps.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (0, -1.6)
(-1.2, 0) -> a -> (1.2, 0) fermion
(1.2, -0.8) -> (-1.2, -0.8) fermion
(-1.2, -1.6) -> b -> (1.2, -1.6) fermion
a -- b screened over "W" pos=0.25
```

## Points without names

A line can end at a position instead of a vertex: `(x, y)`, as in `(-1.5, 0) -> v`. Nothing is drawn there.
Written `+(dx, dy)`, the point is measured from the other end of the same line, so it moves with that end:

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (2, 0)
+(-1, 0.9) -> a fermion "e^-" above
a -> +(-1, -0.9) fermion "e^+" below
a -- b photon "\gamma" above
b -> +(1, 0.9) fermion "\mu^-" above
+(1, -0.9) -> b fermion "\mu^+" below
```

Move `b` and its two lines go with it. One end of a line must be a vertex or a fixed point, so in a chain
such as `a -> +(1, 0) -> +(1, 0)` the second piece is an error.

## Anchors

`K.nw` is a point on the edge of vertex `K` rather than its centre, and a line drawn to an anchor ends
exactly there. On a box, the corner names `nw`, `ne`, `sw` and `se` are ports: points on a face, 0.2 units in
from that corner. A line that arrives more horizontally enters the side face, one that arrives more
vertically the top or bottom face. `n`, `s`, `e` and `w` are the middles of the faces.

```feynman source
K at (0, 0) box "K"
(-1.3, 0.45) -> K.nw fermion
K.ne -> (1.3, 0.45) fermion
(1.3, -0.45) -> K.se fermion
K.sw -> (-1.3, -0.45) fermion
```

On a blob, a disk or any other round vertex, `n`, `ne`, `e` and the rest are compass points on its rim, and a
number is an angle in degrees, anticlockwise from the right: `L.150`. Here the angles bring four lines in
level:

```feynman source
L at (0, 0) blob "L" r=0.6
(-1.6, 0.3) -> L.150 fermion
L.210 -> (-1.6, -0.3) fermion
L.30 -> (1.6, 0.3) fermion
(1.6, -0.3) -> L.330 fermion
```

## Legs

`leg a left` draws an external line from vertex `a`. After the vertex come a direction, a length in units
(0.6 by default), a style (`fermion` by default), `in` or `out` for an arrow into or out of the vertex,
options and a label. Without `in` or `out`, a leg has no arrow. The direction is a word (`left`, `right`,
`up`, `down`, `ne`, `nw`, `se`, `sw`) or an angle in degrees, anticlockwise from the right.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (1.6, 0)
leg a left 1 in "n\mathbf{k}" below
a -> b fermion "n'\mathbf{k}" below
leg b right 1 out "m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" below
leg a 120 1.2 photon "\hbar\omega"
leg b 60 1.2 phonon "\mathbf{q}\nu"
```

<Callout type="warn">
The first number after the vertex is the direction: `leg a 1.2` is a leg at 1.2°. Write the direction
first, then the length: `leg a left 1.2`.
</Callout>

A leg is written from its left end to its right end, or upwards when it is vertical, whatever `in` or `out`
says. So its label goes above it (on the left of a vertical leg), and `side=`, `mom=` and `momdir=` count
along that direction. `above` and `below` come after the direction.

## Self-loops

A line from a vertex to itself is a loop. `above` or `below` says where it goes, or `dir=` gives a direction
word or an angle (up by default). `size=` is its diameter in units (1 by default). The flag `loop` may be
written but is not needed when both ends are the same vertex.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (0, 1)
leg a left 1 in; leg a right 1 out
a -- b coulomb "v" side=right
b -> b fermion loop size=1.2 "G"
```

A loop runs anticlockwise; `cw` turns it clockwise, which reverses its arrow. Its label goes outside it, and
`side=in` puts the label inside. On a vertex that lines stop at, such as a disk or a hatched vertex, the loop
starts inside the shape so that it meets the edge evenly:

```feynman source
v at (0, 0) hatched "g^{DW}"
leg v left 0.7; leg v right 0.7
v -- v phonon dressed loop above size=1.2 "F"
```

## Text, captions and titles

`text "..." at x,y` writes a label anywhere in the diagram, centred on y. `size=` sets its size in px (14
by default), `color=` its colour, `anchor=start`, `middle` or `end` which end of the text sits at x (`middle`
by default), and `upright` sets every letter upright.

`caption "..."` writes a caption under the diagram, or under its term in an equation. `title "..."` sets the
accessible description that screen readers announce, with its TeX read out as text (`G^0` as `G sup 0`).
Without a title, the diagram describes itself from its lines and their labels or, when it has no lines, from its
vertices ([Accessibility](/docs/guides/how-it-works#accessibility)).

```feynman source
a at (0, 0) charge; b at (2.4, 0) charge
(-1, 0) -- a coulomb "v"
b -- (3.4, 0) coulomb "v"
a -> b fermion bulge=0.7 above "G"
b -> a fermion bulge=0.7 below "G"
text "P^0" at 1.2, 0 color=muted
caption "the polarization P^0 between two Coulomb lines"
title "Polarization bubble between two Coulomb lines"
```

A caption is set as text: upright, with every space kept, and with the [label](#labels) commands. The fence
meta can give a caption and a title too ([Remark plugin](/docs/reference/remark-plugin#fence-meta)).

## Equations

A line that starts with an operator begins the next term of an equation: `=`, `+`, `-`, `×`, `≈`, `≡`, `→`
and `:=`, among others ([the full list](/docs/reference/language#equations)). The rest of that line, and the
lines after it, are the statements of the new term. Each term is a diagram of its own, with its own vertex
names, and its y = 0 sits on the line where the operators are set. `...` as a term of its own draws ⋯.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -- (1.6, 0) screened "W"
= (0, 0) -- (1.6, 0) coulomb "v"
+ P at (1.6, 0) bubble "P"
  (0, 0) -- P coulomb "v"
  P -- (3.2, 0) coulomb "v"
+ ...
```

An operator is printed as written, except `-`, which is set as a minus sign (−). `op "..."` sets any text as
the operator, printed exactly as written, not as TeX:

```feynman source
S at (0, 0) blob "\Sigma"
leg S left 0.6; leg S right 0.6
op "= i" a at (0, 0); b at (2.4, 0)
  leg a left 0.6; leg b right 0.6
  a -> b fermion dressed "G" below
  a -- b screened bulge=1 above "W"
```

A line that starts with `|` sets the next diagram beside the last, with no operator. A line that starts with
`\\`, as in TeX, starts a new row; an operator may follow it on the same line.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (1.4, 0) fermion "G"
caption "electron"
| (0, 0) -- (1.4, 0) phonon "F"
caption "phonon"
\\ (0, 0) -- (1.4, 0) photon "D"
caption "photon"
| (0, 0) -- (1.4, 0) screened "W"
caption "screened Coulomb"
```

Each operator stays with the term after it when a long equation wraps. Templates are terms like any other,
and a template that draws a whole equation, such as `dyson`, stands alone in its term
([Using templates](/docs/guides/templates)).

## Labels

Labels are written in double quotes, in a small subset of TeX:

- `_` and `^` set a subscript and a superscript: one character, or a group in braces, as in `G^0`,
  `\Sigma^{ep}` and `G^0_{n\mathbf{k}}`.
- Greek letters by name: `\alpha` to `\omega`, the capitals TeX has (`\Gamma` to `\Omega`), and
  `\varepsilon`, `\varphi`, `\vartheta`.
- `\mathbf{k}` for a bold vector, `\mathrm{}` or `\text{}` for upright text, `\mathit{}` for italic, and
  `\boldsymbol{}` for bold Greek.
- Accents: `\bar{v}`, `\overline{ph}` (one bar over the whole group), `\tilde`, `\hat`, `\vec`, `\dot`.
- Symbols such as `\dagger`, `\uparrow`, `\downarrow`, `\parallel`, `\hbar`, `\infty`, `\pm`, `\times`,
  `\cdot`, `\to`, `\langle` and `\rangle`.
- Spaces: `\,` is a thin space and `\;` a medium one. A space you type is kept.

Latin letters are italic, capital Greek letters upright and lower-case Greek letters italic; digits and symbols
are upright. Latin letters in sub- and superscripts are upright, because they are mostly names (cn, ep, irr);
`\mathit{}` makes one italic. Lower-case Greek letters stay italic there too.

As in TeX, `-` becomes a minus sign and `'` a prime, and a space after a command only ends it: `\delta G` is
δG. `+`, `-` and `=` between two operands get a little space on each side, so `\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}` reads
**k** + **q**. A subscript right after a superscript or a prime is set under it: `\rho^*_{n\mathbf{k}}`,
`G'_z`. A command outside the subset is printed as written.

```feynman source
(0, 0) -> (2.6, 0) fermion "G^0_{n\mathbf{k}}(\omega)"
(3.6, 0) -> (6.2, 0) fermion "m,\,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}"
(0, -1.1) -- (2.6, -1.1) phonon "F_{\mathbf{q}\nu}"
(3.6, -1.1) -- (6.2, -1.1) photon "D_{\mu\nu}"
(0, -2.2) -- (2.6, -2.2) coulomb "\bar{v}"
(3.6, -2.2) -- (6.2, -2.2) screened "W^{e,\mathrm{cn}}"
r at (1.3, -3.2) charge "\rho^*_{n\mathbf{k}}" pos=right
s at (4.9, -3.2) "\mathbf{r}'_1" pos=right
```

The [reference](/docs/reference/language#labels) lists every command.

## Your own line styles

`style D = photon` gives a line style a name of your own, here `D`, which the block can then use wherever a
style goes: `a -- b D "D"`. A style says how a line looks (`dressed`, `heavy`, `color=` and the like), never
where it goes or what it says. [Notation](/docs/guides/notation) shows the looks a style can hold, and
[Site-wide notation and unit](/docs/installation#site-wide-notation-and-unit) how to name styles for every page.

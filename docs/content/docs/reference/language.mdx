This page lists what the parser accepts, in tables. [Writing diagrams](/docs/guides/writing-diagrams)
introduces the same language with examples, and the [template reference](/docs/reference/templates) lists the
templates and their options.

## Reading a block

- A block is read line by line. A statement ends at the end of its line, or at a `;` outside double quotes
  and brackets.
- `%` and `//` start a comment that runs to the end of the line, except inside double quotes.
- Spaces at the start of a line are ignored. Words are separated by spaces.
- Text in double quotes is a label. `\"` puts a double quote in it; every other backslash is kept, for TeX.
- Numbers are written `2`, `-0.5` or `.5`. A position is `(x, y)` or `x,y`, in units: x to the right, y up.
- A name starts with a letter or `_`, and goes on with letters, digits, `_` and primes (`'` or `′`); Greek
  letters count as letters, and `-` may join two parts (`a-1`). A name cannot hold a `.`, which marks an
  anchor.
- `caption`, `title`, `text`, `leg`, `unit` and `style` begin statements of their own, so they cannot name
  vertices. `grid` can, as long as `at` or a line operator follows it.
- `unit`, `grid` and `style` apply to the whole block, wherever they stand. The other statements belong to the
  term they are written in, in order: a vertex must be placed before a `leg`, a label or a restyle names it,
  but a line may name a vertex placed later in the same term.

## Statements

Square brackets mark optional parts. After the position of a vertex and after the ends of a line, the parts may
come in any order; a leg's direction and length come first.

| Statement | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertex | `NAME at x,y [shape] [options] ["label"]` | `S at (2, 0) blob "\Sigma"` |
| Existing vertex | `NAME [shape] [options] ["label"]` | `a current "\mathbf{r}" pos=sw` |
| Line | `END OP END [style] [flags] [options] ["label"]` | `b -> a fermion dressed "G" below` |
| Chain | `END OP END OP END … [style] [flags] [options]` | `v0 -> v1 -> v2 fermion` |
| Self-loop | `NAME OP NAME [style] [flags] [options] ["label"]` | `v -- v phonon loop above size=1.2 "F"` |
| Leg | `leg NAME [direction] [length] [style] [in or out] [flags] [options] ["label"]` | `leg a left 1 in "n\mathbf{k}" below` |
| Text | `text "…" at x,y [size=…] [color=…] [anchor=…] [upright]` | `text "g^{DW}" at 0,-0.5 size=12 color=muted` |
| Caption | `caption "…"` | `caption "Fan–Migdal"` |
| Title | `title "…"` | `title "Fock exchange self-energy"` |
| Template | `NAME [flags] [key=value …] ["label"]` | `ladder n=4 pp rung=phonon` |
| Unit | `unit N` | `unit 36` |
| Grid | `grid` | `grid` |
| Style | `style NAME = BASE [looks]` | `style F = phonon dressed` |
| Operator | `OP [statements]`, at the start of a line | `+ debye-waller dressed` |

An existing vertex, a template's included, takes a new shape, options or label without moving; options it
had before are kept unless given again. `NAME at x,y` for a name already in use places a new vertex, shape and
label included. A template takes a quoted string as its `label=` when it has that option.

## Line ends

| End | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `a` | vertex `a`. A line stops at the edge of a `disk`, `hatched`, `open`, `otimes`, `blob`, `bubble`, `box`, `triangle` or `lens`. Every line but a single solid one also stops at the rim of a small dot or square; a single solid line runs to its centre |
| `a.ne` | an anchor on vertex `a`: the line ends exactly there |
| `(x, y)` | a point, in units; nothing is drawn there |
| `+(dx, dy)` | a point measured from the other end of the same line; one end at least must be a vertex or a point |

A triangle stops lines at the circle through its corners. To run a line up to one of its sides, end it at the
triangle's centre, `T.c`: the triangle is drawn over the line and hides its end.

### Anchors

| Anchor | On a box | On any other vertex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nw`, `ne`, `sw`, `se` | a port: a point on a face, 0.2 units in from that corner (less on a small box). A line that arrives more horizontally uses the side face, one that arrives more vertically the top or bottom face | the rim at 135°, 45°, 225° and 315° |
| `n`, `e`, `s`, `w` | the middle of that face | the rim at 90°, 0°, 270° and 180° |
| `c`, `center` | the centre | the centre |
| a number | the edge in that direction | the rim at that angle |

Angles are in degrees, anticlockwise from the right: `S.30`, `S.-45`. The rim of a lens is its outline, and a
triangle's lies on the circle through its corners. An arc that ends on a box port uses the face its tangent
points through, so an arc over the top of a box leaves from the top face. An unknown anchor is an error.

## Lines

### Line operators

| Operator | Arrow | Style when none is given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `->` | from the first end to the second | `fermion` |
| `<-` | from the second end to the first | `fermion` |
| `--` | none | `plain` |

A chain (`a -> b -> c`) draws one line per operator, each with its own arrow and all with the same style,
flags and options. A chain takes no label.

### Line styles

| Style | Drawn as | Colour | Takes an arrow | `dressed` gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fermion` | solid | `electron` | yes | `fermion2`, a double line |
| `phonon` | zigzag | `phonon` | no | `phonon2`, heavy |
| `photon` | wave | `photon` | no | `photon2`, heavy |
| `coulomb` | dashed | `photon` | no | `screened` |
| `screened` | double dashed | `photon` | no | |
| `field` | wave over dashes | `photon` | no | `field2`, heavy |
| `gluon` | coil | `c5` | no | `gluon2`, heavy |
| `scalar` | dashed | `ink` | yes | `scalar2`, double |
| `ghost` | dotted | `ink` | yes | |
| `plain` | solid | `ink` | yes | `plain2`, double |
| `boson` | wave | `ink` | yes | `boson2`, heavy |
| `impurity` | dashed, with a cross | `muted` | no | |

The forms ending in 2 are styles of their own as well, and `bare` turns them (and `screened`) back. Other
names: `electron` and `hole` for `fermion`, `electron2` for `fermion2`, `higgs` for `scalar`, and
`interaction` for `coulomb`. A name given with `style`, in the block or by the site, works wherever a style
does. An arrow on a line whose style takes none is an error: draw it with `--`.

### Line options

| Option | Value | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `label=` | text | | the label; a quoted string does the same |
| `side=` | `left`, `right`, `in`, `out` | `left`; `out` on an arc or loop | where the label goes: left or right of the written direction, or inside or outside an arc or loop |
| `pos=` | 0 to 1 | halfway between the vertices | where the label sits along the line |
| `dist=` | px | a few px past the line's reach, more beside an arrowhead | the label's distance from the line |
| `bend=` | number | 0 | an arc whose height is this fraction of the distance between the ends; 0.5 is a half circle |
| `bulge=` | units, 0 or more | | an arc this many units high; it needs `above` or `below` |
| `dir=` | a direction | `up` | where a loop goes |
| `size=` | units | 1 | the diameter of a loop |
| `mom=` | text | | a momentum arrow beside the line, with this label |
| `momside=` | `left`, `right` | opposite the label; on a straight line with no label, the side `above` or `below` names | the side of the written direction the momentum arrow goes on |
| `momdir=` | `back` | along the written direction | turns the momentum arrow round |
| `mompos=` | 0 to 1 | beside the label | where the momentum arrow sits along the line |
| `color=` | a colour | the style's | colour of the line, its arrowhead and its label |
| `width=` | px | 1.8; heavy 3.2 | stroke width |
| `amp=` | px | 3.6; coil 4; heavy zigzag 4.2 | amplitude of a wave, zigzag or coil |
| `wl=` | px | 9; zigzag 7; heavy zigzag 9.5 | wavelength of a wave, zigzag or coil |
| `dash=` | `"dash gap"` in px | `"5 4"` | dash pattern of a dashed line |
| `arrow=` | `mid`, `end`, `start`, `none` | `mid` | where the arrowhead goes |
| `arrowpos=` | 0 to 1 | halfway between the vertices | where a middle arrowhead sits along the line |
| `marker=` | `cross`, `none` | `cross` on `impurity` | a cross in the middle of the line |
| `opacity=` | 0 to 1 | | opacity of the line |

- The written direction runs from a line's first end to its second, whatever its arrow says. Left of it is the
  viewer's left, so the label of a line written left to right goes above it.
- `pos=`, `mompos=` and `arrowpos=` count along the line as drawn, from its first end (0) to its second (1).
  On a loop, 0 and 1 are at the vertex and 0.5 is opposite it.
- `bend=` takes its sign from the written direction: a positive value bulges to the left. With `above` or
  `below` it may not be negative, and the page word gives the side.
- `bulge=` may be at most half the distance between the ends; `arc` is that half circle.
- `arrow=end` puts the arrowhead at the end the arrow points to, with its tip on that end. `arrow=start` puts it
  at the end the arrow leaves, inside the line: its back sits on that end and it points along the line.
  `arrow=none` removes it, and `arrow=mid` is the default.
- `dash=` changes only dashed styles. The dashes are stretched so that the line starts and ends on a dash.
- `momside=`, `momdir=` and `mompos=` place the momentum arrow of `mom=`, and without `mom=` they are an error.
  On a loop, the momentum arrow goes outside, a sixth of the way round from a label outside.
  [Momentum](/docs/guides/momentum) has the details.
- `dir=` and `size=` act on loops only.

### Line flags

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `above`, `below` | a side of the page, whatever order the ends are written in. On a straight line, where the label goes; on an arc, the side it bulges to; on a loop, where the loop goes. Only on lines within 45° of horizontal |
| `bend` | an arc with `bend=0.35` |
| `arc` | an arc with `bend=0.5`, a half circle |
| `dressed` | the style's dressed form |
| `bare` | the style's bare form |
| `heavy` | a heavy stroke |
| `thin` | a thin stroke |
| `double` | two strands |
| `faint` | drawn at 35% opacity |
| `over` | the line passes over the lines written before it: each line it crosses gets a gap, except a dashed or dotted one, which keeps its own gaps; a dashed `over` line keeps a dash on each crossing |
| `loop` | a self-loop on the first end; implied when both ends are the same vertex, without anchors |
| `cw` | a loop runs clockwise |
| `ccw` | a loop runs anticlockwise, the default |

Where two wavy, zigzag or coiled lines cross, the later one passes over the earlier one without `over`. An
arrowhead at a crossing moves off it.

## Vertices

### Shapes

| Shape | Drawn as | Size | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dot` | dot in ink (the default) | radius 3 px | below |
| `charge` | larger dot in ink, for ρ | radius 3.4 px | below |
| `current` | teal dot, for j | radius 3.6 px | below |
| `eph` | amber dot, the electron–phonon g | radius 3.6 px | below |
| `dipole` | rose dot, d·E | radius 3.6 px | below |
| `spin` | violet dot, σ·B | radius 3.6 px | below |
| `disk` | grey disk, a screened vertex | radius 6.5 px | below |
| `hatched` | hatched disk | radius 8 px | below |
| `open` | open circle | radius 4.5 px | below |
| `cross` | a cross | radius 4.5 px | below |
| `otimes` | a circled cross | radius 6.5 px | below |
| `square` | small square | 6.8 px wide | below |
| `blob` | grey circle, a self-energy | radius 0.42 units | inside |
| `bubble` | blue circle | radius 0.42 units | inside |
| `box` | rounded box, a four-point vertex | 0.9 × 1.3 units | inside |
| `triangle` | triangle, pointing right | radius 0.46 units | inside |
| `lens` | lens, a response function; upright | 0.8 × 1.4 units | inside |
| `none`, `point` | nothing | | below |

Where a double line ends on a small dot, every dot of that term without an `r=` grows a little, so that both
strands end on its rim. [Vocabulary](/docs/examples/vocabulary#vertex-shapes) draws every shape.

### Vertex options

| Option | Value | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `label=` | text | | the label; a quoted string does the same. A vertex takes one label |
| `pos=` | a position word or an angle | below; inside a blob, bubble, box, triangle or lens | where the label goes |
| `color=` | a colour | the shape's | colour of the shape |
| `r=` | units | the shape's | radius of any shape but a box or lens |
| `w=` | units | box 0.9; lens 0.8 upright, 1.4 lying | width on the page |
| `h=` | units | box 1.3; lens 1.4 upright, 0.8 lying | height on the page |
| `dir=` | a direction | triangle `right`; lens upright | where a triangle points; `left` or `right` lays a lens down |
| `size=` | px | 13 | font size of a label outside the shape |
| `faint` | flag | | drawn at 35% opacity |

`pos=` takes `below`, `above`, `left`, `right`, `ne`, `nw`, `se`, `sw`, `n`, `s`, `e`, `w`, `up`, `down`,
`top`, `bottom` or `center`, or an angle in degrees, anticlockwise from the right (`pos=30`). `n`, `up` and
`top` mean above, `s`, `down` and `bottom` below. On a blob, bubble, box, triangle or lens, any `pos=` but
`center` puts the label outside; other shapes take `center` as below. A label inside a shape is 14 px, one
outside 13 px or `size=`, set 4 px clear of the shape.

The label of a small dot or a `none` vertex is set in the ink colour. Other shapes give their label the text
shade of their colour, or ink for the grey ones. A lens is never thicker than it is long: where `w=` and `h=`
would make it so, its thickness is cut to its length, which draws a circle.

## Legs

| Part | Value | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NAME` | a vertex placed earlier in the term | |
| direction | a [direction](#directions), right after the name | `left` |
| length | units, right after the direction | 0.6 |
| style | a line style | `fermion` |
| `in`, `out` | an arrow into the vertex, or out of it | no arrow |
| flags and options | as on a line, except `arrow=` | |
| label | a quoted string, or `label=` | |

- The first word or number after the name is the direction, unless it is a style, a flag, `in` or `out`:
  `leg a 1.2` points at 1.2°, and `leg a phonon` points left.
- A leg is written from its left end to its right end, or upwards when it is vertical, whatever `in` or `out`
  says. Its label goes above it, or on the left of a vertical leg, and `side=`, `mom=` and `momdir=` count
  along that direction.
- `above` and `below` come after the direction.
- `in` and `out` set the arrow; `arrow=` on a leg is an error.

## Self-loops

A line whose two ends are the same vertex, without anchors, is a loop. `loop` on a line between two different
vertices is an error.

| Part | Value | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `above`, `below` | the loop goes above or below the vertex | |
| `dir=` | a [direction](#directions) | `up` |
| `size=` | units | 1 |
| `cw`, `ccw` | the way it runs | anticlockwise |
| `side=` | `in`, `out`; without `above` or `below`, also `left`, `right` | `out` |
| `pos=` | 0 to 1, round the loop from the vertex | 0.5, opposite the vertex |
| `mom=` | as on a line | outside the loop |

On a vertex that lines stop at, the loop starts inside the shape and is cut at its edge. A loop takes no
`bulge=` or `bend=`, and with `above` or `below` no `dir=`. A `->` arrow runs the way the loop runs, a `<-`
arrow against it.

## Text

| Part | Value | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `"…"` | the text, typeset as a label | |
| `at x,y` | a position | |
| `size=` | px | 14 |
| `color=` | a colour | ink |
| `anchor=` | `start`, `middle`, `end` | `middle` |
| `upright` | flag | off |

The text is centred on y, and `anchor=` says which end of it sits at x. A particle colour gives its text
shade. `upright` sets every letter upright.

## Captions and titles

- `caption "…"` writes a caption under the diagram, or under its term in an equation. It is set as text:
  upright, every space kept, with the label commands. For a single diagram, a `caption=` in the fence meta
  replaces it.
- `title "…"` sets the accessible description, the SVG's `aria-label`, with its TeX read out as text: `G^0`
  becomes `G sup 0`. Without one, a template gives its own, read the same way, and any other diagram is described
  by its lines and their labels or, with no lines, by its vertices (`blob Σ, box K`). A `title=` in the fence
  meta, used as written, describes the whole figure instead
  ([Accessibility](/docs/guides/how-it-works#accessibility)).

## Global statements

| Statement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `unit N` | px per unit for the whole block, a positive number; 40 by default. It wins over a `unit=` in the fence meta and over the [site's unit](/docs/installation#site-wide-notation-and-unit). With several, the last counts. Strokes, arrowheads and labels keep their size in px |
| `grid` | on its own, with no options: draws the unit grid under every term, marks y = 0 and writes each vertex's name over the drawing |
| `style NAME = BASE [looks]` | names a line style; the `=` may be left out |

A style says how a line looks. Its looks are `dressed`, `bare`, `heavy`, `thin`, `double`, `faint`, `color=`,
`width=`, `amp=`, `wl=`, `dash=`, `marker=` and `opacity=`, with the values they take on a line. Anything else
(`above`, `bend=`, `mom=`, a label) is an error, since where a line goes and what it says belong to the line.

`BASE` is a line style, another name for one, or a style of the block or the site. A style may be used
before its `style` statement. An unknown base is an error where the style is used. Options written on a line
win over its style's. A template option that takes a line style keeps the style's looks: after
`style F = phonon dressed`, `fan-migdal phonon=F` draws the dressed phonon. Styles for the whole site are set up
in [Site-wide notation and unit](/docs/installation#site-wide-notation-and-unit).

## Equations

A line that starts with an operator begins a new term. The rest of that line and the lines after it, up to
the next operator, are the term's statements.

| Operator | Note |
| --- | --- |
| `=`, `:=` | |
| `+` | not before `(`: `+(dx, dy)` is a relative point |
| `−` | the minus sign, U+2212 |
| `-` | set as the minus sign, −; not before `-`, `>`, a digit or `.` |
| `×`, `·` | |
| `≈`, `≃`, `≡`, `∝` | |
| `→`, `⇒` | |
| `\|` | no operator: the next diagram goes beside the last |
| `op "…"` | any text |

- Operators are printed as written, not typeset as TeX, except `-`, which is set as −. `op` text is printed
  exactly as written, a `-` in it included.
- An operator on the first line stays in front of the first term. An operator with no term after it is an
  error.
- `...`, `…`, `⋯`, `\cdots` or `\ldots` as a term's only statement draws ⋯.
- `\\` at the start of a line starts a new row, and an operator may follow it on the same line. A `\\` alone
  on its line starts the row at the next term.
- An equation template (`dyson`, `hedin-vertex`, `bse`, `tmatrix`, `parquet`, `gt-bse`) must stand alone in
  its term. Its terms join the equation, and an operator written before it replaces its first.
- Each term has its own vertices: a name in one term does not reach another. A term's y = 0 sits on the line
  where the operators are set, and its caption under it.

## Colours

`color=` takes a colour name of the theme, or any CSS colour.

| Name | Also | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `electron` | `fermion`, `blue` | fermion lines, bubbles, lenses |
| `phonon` | `amber`, `orange` | phonon lines, `eph` |
| `photon` | `rose`, `pink` | photon, Coulomb, screened and field lines, `dipole` |
| `c4` | `teal`, `current` | `current` |
| `c5` | `violet`, `purple` | gluon lines, `spin` |
| `ink` | `fg` | plain, scalar, ghost and boson lines, dots, labels |
| `muted` | `grey`, `gray` | disks, blobs, boxes, triangles, impurity lines, momentum arrows |

Any other value is passed to CSS as it stands: `color="#c2410c"`, `color=var(--brand)`. Labels use the darker

### Spaces and escapes

- `\,` is a thin space, `\;` and `\:` a medium space, and `\!` nothing. A backslash before a space gives a
  space.
- `\{`, `\}`, `\_`, `\^`, `\%`, `\#`, `\&` and `\\` give the character itself, and `\|` gives ‖.
- A command outside the subset is printed as written, upright. A stray `}` is left out.

### How labels are set

- Latin letters are italic, capital Greek letters upright and lower-case Greek letters italic. Digits,
  punctuation and symbols are upright.
- In sub- and superscripts, Latin letters are upright, as names such as cn, ep and irr are. `\mathit{}` makes
  them italic; the `scriptItalic` option of the [component](/docs/reference/component) makes them italic in
  every label.
- `-` becomes a minus sign, `'` a prime and `*` an asterisk operator (∗).
- `+`, `-`, `=`, `×`, `±`, `∓`, `·`, `<`, `>`, `≤`, `≥`, `≈` and `≡` between two operands get a medium space on
  each side, so `\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}` reads **k** + **q**. A sign gets none: `-q`.
- A space after a command only ends it, so `\delta G` is δG. Beside an operator or a relation the space stays:
  `\Sigma = x`, `A \times B`. Every other space is kept as typed.
- In text (captions, `\text{}`, `\textrm{}`, `\textit{}`, `\textbf{}`, `\operatorname{}`) every space is
  kept, and `-`, `'` and `*` stay as typed.

## Errors

A diagram with an error is not drawn. Each error names the line of the block it is on, and an error in what a
template draws names the template's line: the page shows the errors in a box, and the build reports them against
the line of the `.mdx` file ([Errors](/docs/guides/errors)). These are errors:

**Statements**

- A statement that is none of those above. A single unknown word gets the list of templates, and the nearest
  one when a template's name is close:
  `unknown template or statement "ladderr"; did you mean ladder? (templates: …)`.
- A name close to a template's, followed by that template's options or flags:
  `unknown template "fok"; did you mean fock?`.
- A label, shape or option for a name that is no vertex of the term yet; a close name is suggested.
- A block with nothing to draw.

**Vertices**

- `at` with no position after it, or a name with a `.` in it.
- A second label.
- An option that is not a vertex option (the nearest is suggested), or a word that is not a shape.
- A `pos=` that is neither a position word nor an angle.
- `above`, `below`, `up`, `down`, `top` or `bottom` as a bare word: write `pos=above`.

**Lines and legs**

- A line that does not start with a vertex or a point, or an operator with no end after it.
- A second style or a second quoted label, or a label on a chain.
- A word that is neither a style nor a flag, on a line or a leg (`leg: unknown word "D" (line styles: …)`),
  or an option that is not a line option (the nearest is suggested). `height=` and `toward=`, from an earlier
  version, name their replacements.
- `above=` or `below=` with a value: they are bare words.
- A value outside what the tables above give for `side=`, `pos=`, `bend=`, `bulge=`, `momside=`, `momdir=`,
  `mompos=`, `arrow=`, `arrowpos=` or `marker=`.
- `momside=`, `momdir=` or `mompos=` without `mom=`.
- `arrow=` on a leg, whatever its value: a leg takes its arrow from `in` or `out`.
- An arrow on a line whose style takes none.
- `loop` on a line between two different vertices.
- `above` with `below`; either of them on a line steeper than 45° or with no length; either on a straight line
  that has no label and no `mom=`, or together with `side=` on a straight line.
- `bulge=` without `above` or `below`, below zero, or more than half the distance between the ends; `bulge=`
  together with `bend=`, `bend` or `arc`; `bend=` below zero with `above` or `below`.
- `side=in` or `side=out` on a straight line; `side=left` or `side=right` on an arc or loop that `above` or
  `below` places.
- On a loop: `bulge=` or `bend=`; `dir=` with `above` or `below`; with `above` or `below`, a `size=` that is
  not positive.
- Two relative ends, or a line that lies wholly inside the vertex at its end.
- An end that names no vertex of the term, or an unknown anchor.
- A style whose base is not a line style.
- A leg on a vertex not yet placed, a leg direction that is not a direction, `above` or `below` before the
  direction, or a second number after the length.

**Text, captions and titles**

- `text` without a quoted string, or without a position after `at`; an option but `size=`, `color=` and
  `anchor=`; an `anchor=` other than `start`, `middle` or `end`; a word but `upright`.
- `caption` or `title` without a quoted string.

**Global statements**

- `grid` followed by anything but `at` or a line operator.
- `unit` without a positive number.
- `style` without a name and a base; a word or option that is not a look, or a look's value out of range.

**Equations and templates**

- An operator with no term after it.
- An equation template sharing its term with other statements.
- A template option the template does not have. The message suggests the nearest when one is close, and lists
  those it has: `ladder: unknown option "rungs"; did you mean rung? (options: n, channel, …)`.
- A number option written bare or without a number (`ladder: n expects a number, as in n=3`), a text option
  without a value, and each template's own checks.

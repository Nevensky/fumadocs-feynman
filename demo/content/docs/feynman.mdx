Write a diagram in a code block whose language is `feynman`, the way fumadocs uses `mermaid` blocks. The remark plugin turns the
block into `<Feynman chart="..." />`, a server component that emits static SVG: no client JavaScript, and the
colours follow the light/dark theme through CSS variables.

Every example below is a block with `source` in its fence: the diagram is followed by what was written. Where that
is a template, the **By hand** tab shows the same diagram written out vertex by vertex, line by line: the place to
start from when no template quite fits.

## Vocabulary

### Line styles

Built-in styles. `dressed` turns each into its "2" form: double for fermions and the Coulomb line, heavy for the others.

```feynman source
(0,0) -> (2.2,0) fermion "fermion"
(3.2,0) -> (5.4,0) fermion2 "fermion2"
(0,-1) -- (2.2,-1) phonon "phonon"
(3.2,-1) -- (5.4,-1) phonon2 "phonon2"
(0,-2) -- (2.2,-2) photon "photon"
(3.2,-2) -- (5.4,-2) photon2 "photon2"
(0,-3) -- (2.2,-3) coulomb "coulomb"
(3.2,-3) -- (5.4,-3) screened "screened"
(0,-4) -- (2.2,-4) field "field"
(3.2,-4) -- (5.4,-4) gluon "gluon"
(0,-5) -- (2.2,-5) scalar "scalar"
(3.2,-5) -> (5.4,-5) ghost "ghost"
```

### Vertex shapes

Lines stop at the edge of disks, blobs and boxes on their own.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0) dot "dot"
b at (1.1, 0) current "current"
c at (2.2, 0) disk "disk"
d at (3.3, 0) hatched "hatched"
e at (4.4, 0) open "open"
f at (5.5, 0) cross "cross"
g at (0.4, -1.5) blob label="\Sigma"
h at (2, -1.5) bubble label="\chi"
i at (3.6, -1.5) box label="\Gamma"
j at (5.2, -1.5) triangle label="\Lambda" dir=left
k at (0, -3) charge "charge"
l at (1.1, -3) eph "eph"
m at (2.2, -3) dipole "dipole"
n at (3.3, -3) spin "spin"
o at (4.8, -3) lens label="\chi" dir=right
```

### Vertices with their matrix elements

Any vertex can be labelled once it exists, a template’s too: write its name, then a label and where it goes (pos=), a shape or a colour. The names are on the By hand tab, and grid shows them. Charge vertices carry ρ, current vertices j; eph, dipole and spin are the other couplings, each in its particle’s hue.

```feynman source
bubble vertex=charge ext=coulomb extLabel=v
a0 "\rho_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G})" pos=sw
b0 "\rho^*_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G}')" pos=se
| bubble vertex=current ext=photon extLabel=D
a0 "j_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G})" pos=sw
b0 "j^*_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{G}')" pos=se
```

### Drafting grid

A line that says grid draws the unit grid and the name of every vertex. Positions are (x, y) in units of 40 px, y up; the main line sits on y = 0, where an equation sets = and +. Delete the line when the diagram is done.

```feynman source
grid
a at (0, 0) disk; b at (2.8, 0) disk
leg a left; leg b right
b -> a fermion dressed "G" below
a -- b phonon dressed bulge=1.2 above "F"
```

## Self-energies

### Fan–Migdal, written by hand

The EPP-primer look: the electron enters on the right, screened vertices are grey disks. below and above name sides of the page, whatever order the ends are written in; bulge=1.2 is the height of the arc in units.

```feynman source
a at (0, 0) disk; b at (2.8, 0) disk
leg a left; leg b right
b -> a fermion dressed "G" below
a -- b phonon dressed bulge=1.2 above "F"
```

### Debye–Waller, written by hand

On a loop, above and below say where it goes; size= is its diameter in units.

```feynman source
v at (0, 0) hatched "g^{DW}" pos=below
leg v left 0.7; leg v right 0.7
v -- v phonon dressed loop above size=1.2 "F"
```

### Rainbow, written by hand

A chain draws three electron lines at once; the outer phonon rises 1.4 units, the inner one is a half circle.

```feynman source
v0 at (0, 0); v1 at (1.15, 0); v2 at (2.3, 0); v3 at (3.45, 0)
leg v0 left 0.55; leg v3 right 0.55
v0 -> v1 -> v2 -> v3 fermion
v0 -- v3 phonon bulge=1.4 above
v1 -- v2 phonon arc above
```

### Σ^ep = Fan–Migdal + Debye–Waller

Templates are terms of an equation: a line that starts with = or + begins the next term.

```feynman source
sigma label="\Sigma^{ep}"
= fan-migdal dressed vertex=disk
+ debye-waller dressed
```

### Hartree, Fock and GW

A line that starts with | sets the next diagram beside the last, with no operator.

```feynman source
hartree
caption "Hartree"
| fock
caption "Fock"
| gw
caption "GW"
```

### Second order: rainbow and crossed

Migdal’s theorem: the crossed diagram is smaller by a factor √(m/M).

```feynman source
sigma2 rainbow
caption "rainbow (non-crossing)"
| sigma2 crossed
caption "crossed (vertex correction)"
```

### Fock and GW, standing up

vertical stands a self-energy up: the interaction runs straight from the lower vertex to the upper one, and the electron arcs round on the right, in at the bottom and out at the top (dir=left reverses it). The interaction carries no arrow, as a phonon or a photon line carries none: mom= draws the momentum it carries as an arrow beside it, down towards the vertex where the electron enters, the way q runs when the inner electron carries k+q; standing up, it also lengthens the interaction a little, to give the arrow room.

```feynman source
fock vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" G="m,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" v="v(\mathbf{q})" mom="\mathbf{q}"
| gw vertical legLabel="n\mathbf{k}" mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

### The momentum through the interaction

mom= puts the momentum beside the interaction, as an arrow that points towards the vertex where the electron enters: the way q runs when the inner electron carries k+q, as in Σ(k) = i Σ\_q v(q) G(k+q). momdir=back turns it round, for k−q. Around an inner diagram both pieces carry it, and momtop= labels the upper one. The arrow sits inside, opposite the label, where a short momentum such as q fits; momside=out puts it outside, for a longer one such as Q+G′∥, and the label makes way. By hand, a line’s arrow runs from its first end to its second: the interaction is written from a, so the arrow towards a reads momdir=back there.

```feynman source
fock legLabel="\mathbf{k}" G="\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" mom="\mathbf{q}"
caption "inner line \mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}"
| fock legLabel="\mathbf{k}" G="\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}" mom="\mathbf{q}" momdir=back
caption "inner line \mathbf{k}−\mathbf{q}: momdir=back"
| fock vertical inner=chi mom="\mathbf{Q}" momtop="\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}'_\parallel" momside=out
caption "momtop=, momside=out"
```

### The correlation self-energy: a response function inside the interaction

inner=chi splits the interaction around a response function χ, so that it reads v χ v = W − v, the correlation part of the screened interaction (with the plain Fock diagram it makes GW). Its points are c and d, the lens is X; here every point carries its plane-wave label.

```feynman source
fock vertical inner=chi legLabel="n,\mathbf{K}" G="m,\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{Q}" v="v(\mathbf{Q})" vtop="v(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}'_\parallel)" chi="\chi(\omega-E_{m\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{Q}})"
a "G_z" pos=below
c "G_{z1}" pos=right
d "G_{z2}" pos=right
b "G'_z" pos=above
```

### A polarization bubble inside, and a ladder inside that

inner=bubble puts the polarization bubble itself into the interaction, two electron lines from c to d: the first term of the RPA screening. ladder= adds W rungs inside it, the electron–hole attraction that excitons come from.

```feynman source
fock vertical inner=bubble
| fock vertical inner=bubble ladder=2 rungLabel=W
```

### Hartree and Fock, their points labelled

The Hartree tadpole and the Fock exchange with the points r and r′ written on their vertices; dir=left runs the electron right to left.

```feynman source
hartree G="-iG(\mathbf{r}';\mathbf{r}',0^+)" legLabel=n
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=sw
caption "H"
+ fock vertical dir=left G=m legLabel=n
a "\mathbf{r}" pos=below
b "\mathbf{r}'" pos=above
caption "F"
```

## Hedin’s expansion and GT

### Hedin’s self-energy to third order in W

Müller, Blügel and Friedrich, PRB 100, 045130 (2019), Fig. 1. First row: GW; the second-order screened exchange (the first vertex correction, also called G3W2); the two direct third-order diagrams, a particle–hole and a particle–particle ladder closed by G: the lowest terms of the two T-matrix self-energies beyond Hartree and GW (their one- and two-rung terms, the tadpole and the second-order direct diagram, are already there, which is why the paper’s T starts at third order; its GT keeps the particle–hole one, with opposite spins on its rails). Second row: the four third-order exchange diagrams. G and W are the full propagators, so the lines are double: the expansion is in G and W, which is why no diagram carries a self-energy insertion (no rainbow). arcs numbers the vertices from the left and puts each W above or below the line; dir=left runs the electron right to left, as in the paper; a line that starts with `\\` begins a new row, as in TeX.

```feynman source
gw G="" W="" dir=left
| arcs above="13 24" dressed dir=left
| gt ladder spin=false dressed dir=left
| gt ladder pp spin=false dressed dir=left
\\ arcs above="15 24" below="36" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="13 46" below="25" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="26 35" below="14" dressed dir=left
| arcs above="14 36" below="25" dressed dir=left
```

### GT drawn like GW: the magnon in place of the plasmon

Fig. 2 of the same paper. W carries plasmons (and electron–hole pairs); the T matrix, a ladder of W between an electron and a hole of opposite spin, carries magnons (spin waves and Stoner excitations). Read as in GW, with T as the boson, the electron that emits one flips its spin (↓ on the base line) and flips back when it absorbs it; following the arrows, the ↑ line runs on along the outer rail and the ↓ base line closes a loop with the inner rail. Single lines: the paper evaluates GT with the Kohn–Sham G.

```feynman source
gw dressed=false G="\uparrow" dir=left
| gt n=5 label="GT" dir=left
```

### The T matrix in the ladder approximation

Fig. 3: T starts at third order in W, T = WKWKW + WKT with K(12,34) = iG(13)G(42) the free electron–hole pair, and GT closes it with the G from 4 back to 2, Σ(13) = −i T(12,34) G(24). (The paper’s Eq. 1 prints G(42); with its K the line that closes the loop is G(24), as its Fig. 3 draws.) Lines joined only by W carry opposite spins. The violet box is the T matrix.

```feynman source
gt-bse dir=left
```

### GT as a box and as a ladder; the particle–particle T matrix

form = arch (the default), box or ladder. For a particle–particle T matrix (pp) the two rails run the same way, and the line that closes it runs against both: a hole when the pair is two electrons, an electron when it is two holes. A pp T matrix carries no magnon.

```feynman source
gt box
| gt ladder
| gt pp
```

## Trions and biexcitons

### Exciton, trion, biexciton: ladders of two, three and four lines

The exciton is a bound state of the two-line ladder, the screened direct part (W) of the Bethe–Salpeter kernel; its exchange part v̄, in which the pair annihilates into a bare Coulomb line, is not drawn. A trion adds a third line, a biexciton a fourth. The rungs (W) now join every pair of lines, not only neighbours: a rung that skips a line passes over it, and the gap shows it. Arrows run with the electrons and against the holes, so one W serves for all pairs: e–h attracts, e–e and h–h repel.

```feynman source
nbody
caption "exciton"
| trion
caption "trion X^{−}"
| biexciton
caption "biexciton"
```

### Negative and positive trions

rungs lists the columns left to right as pairs of lines, counted from the top: 13 joins the two electrons (or the two holes) over the line between them. Not drawn: exchange. Two identical particles (same spin and valley) also need the same ladders with the outgoing ends of their two lines swapped, each with a minus sign; and an electron–hole pair that can annihilate (its electron has the spin of the missing one) also has the exciton’s exchange term v̄. Which exchange terms a trion has depends on its spins and valleys, and they, electron–hole and electron–electron together, set its fine structure.

```feynman source
trion
caption "X^{−} = e h e"
| trion positive
caption "X^{+} = h e h"
```

### Biexciton: two electrons and two holes, every pair interacting

12+34 puts two rungs in one column. Rungs 12 and 34 bind each exciton. Between the excitons, 13 and 24 (electron–electron, hole–hole) repel and 23 and 14 attract; at first order they nearly cancel. Summed to all orders, with each electron also correlating with the other hole, they bind the two excitons, as in H₂, when the two electrons (and the two holes) are not in the same spin and valley state: a spin singlet within one valley, or two different valleys whatever the spins. For two electrons of the same spin and valley the exchange diagrams, not drawn, give Pauli repulsion instead.

```feynman source
biexciton
```

### Your own: an exciton scattering off an electron

nbody takes any lines (e or h, from the top) and any rungs. Here an exciton (lines 1 and 2) meets the electron on line 3, which attracts its hole (23) and repels its electron (13), and goes on.

```feynman source
nbody lines="e h e" rungs="12 12 23 13 12 12"
```

## Vertex corrections

### First order: a phonon or W across the vertex

Left, the electron–photon vertex dressed by a phonon; right, the Coulomb vertex dressed by W. Migdal’s theorem, which makes phonon vertex corrections small, holds at large momentum transfer (v\_F q ≫ ω); a photon brings q ≈ 0, where it fails. There the Ward identity fixes the charge vertex, Γ = 1 + λ, as large as the self-energy’s renormalization, but not the current vertex a photon couples to: its phonon correction carries the cosine of the scattering angle, vanishes for isotropic coupling, and in transport turns the quasiparticle lifetime into the transport one.

```feynman source
vertex-correction ext=photon rung=phonon rungLabel=F
| vertex-correction ext=coulomb rung=screened rungLabel=W
```

### Second order, both W across the vertex: ladder and crossed

n sets the number of interactions across the vertex; crossed crosses them. Hedin’s vertex has two more second-order diagrams of this kind, in which one W crosses the vertex and the other sits on one leg, crossing it; put into Σ = iGWΓ, the four give the four third-order exchange diagrams of Hedin’s expansion.

```feynman source
vertex-correction n=2 ext=screened rung=screened rungLabel=W
caption "ladder"
| vertex-correction n=2 crossed ext=screened rung=screened
caption "crossed"
```

### Hedin’s vertex: Γ = 1 + (δΣ/δG) GGΓ

The Bethe–Salpeter equation for the vertex function. With Σ = iGW, δΣ/δG = iW + iG δW/δG. Dropping δW/δG leaves Γ = 1 + iWGGΓ, whose first iteration is the first-order correction above; the δW/δG term is where the direct (closed-ladder) diagrams of Hedin’s expansion come from.

```feynman source
hedin-vertex
```

### The vertex in Σ and P: Σ = iGWΓ, P = −iGGΓ

gamma= on gw and bubble closes the diagram with Hedin’s vertex instead of a bare one; Γ = 1 gives back GW and the RPA bubble. In Σ(12) = i G(13) W(1⁺4) Γ(32;4) the electron enters through Γ and leaves at the bare vertex.

```feynman source
gw gamma="\Gamma"
caption "\Sigma = iGW\Gamma"
| bubble gamma="\Gamma" top=G bottom=G dressed ext=screened
caption "P = −iGG\Gamma"
```

## Written by hand

### Hedin–Baym: W = Wᵉ + Wᵉ χⁱᵒⁿ Wᵉ

```feynman source
(0, 0) -- (1.8, 0) screened "W" above
= (0, 0) -- (1.8, 0) screened "W^{e}" above
+ p3 at (1.5, 0); p4 at (3.3, 0)
  (0, 0) -- p3 screened "W^{e}" above
  p3 -- p4 phonon dressed "\chi^{ion}" above
  p4 -- (4.8, 0) screened "W^{e}" above
```

### Electron–phonon vertex with crystal momenta

A vertical line has no above or below: side=right counts along the written direction, here upwards, so it is the page’s right.

```feynman source
v at (0, 0)
(-1.5, 0) -> v fermion "n\mathbf{k}" above
v -> (1.5, 0) fermion "m\,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" above
(0, -1.3) -- v phonon "\mathbf{q}\nu" side=right mom="\mathbf{q}"
```

### Phonon-assisted absorption

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (1.6, 0)
leg a left 1 fermion in "n\mathbf{k}" below
a -> b fermion "n'\mathbf{k}" below
leg b right 1 fermion out "m\,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}" below
(-0.7, 1.1) -- a photon "\hbar\omega"
b -- (2.3, 1.1) phonon "\mathbf{q}\nu"
```

### Other fields: e⁺e⁻ → μ⁺μ⁻ and a gluon

```feynman source
a at (0, 0); b at (2, 0)
(-1, 0.9) -> a fermion "e^-" above
a -> (-1, -0.9) fermion "e^+" below
a -- b photon "\gamma" above
b -> (3, 0.9) fermion "\mu^-" above
(3, -0.9) -> b fermion "\mu^+" below
| (0, 0) -- (2, 0) gluon "g" above
```

### Your own notation

style names how a line looks, once; the remark plugin also takes styles for the whole site. Where a line goes stays on the line.

```feynman source
style D = photon
style F = phonon dressed
a at (0, 0) current; b at (2.4, 0) current
leg a left D; leg b right D
a -> b fermion dressed bulge=1.1 above "G"
b -> a fermion dressed bulge=1.1 below "G"
a -- b F "F"
```

## Fence options and the component

`source` in the fence meta shows the block's source under the diagram, with a By hand tab when it uses a template:

````md
```feynman source
fock vertical
```
````

A caption from the fence meta:

```feynman caption="The Fan–Migdal self-energy, with screened vertices"
fan-migdal dressed vertex=disk
```

The component can also be used directly:
